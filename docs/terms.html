Terms of service

These terms set out what ElitePropX does, what you are responsible for, how the flat fee works, when a retry applies, and where the risk sits. They are written to be read rather than to be skimmed past.

The short version

  • We trade your prop firm evaluation and hand over the funded account. Nothing else.
  • Fee: $220 flat per evaluation, any account size, retries on rule breaches included.
  • You must check your firm's rules on third-party trading before you buy. That risk is yours.
  • We never take a share of payouts and never trade funded accounts.

1. Agreement

These terms govern the relationship between you and ElitePropX in respect of a prop firm challenge-passing engagement. By paying the fee you confirm that you have read them and that you accept them. If you do not accept them, do not engage the service — nothing is charged before you decide.

An engagement begins when the fee has been paid and the firm, account size and rule set have been confirmed in writing. At that point the specific engagement terms — firm, size, fee, timeline range, retry conditions and any compliance caveat about your firm — are recorded in the email thread between us, and those recorded terms form part of this agreement. Where the email record and this page conflict, the email record governs, because it reflects what was actually agreed for your evaluation.

2. What the service is

ElitePropX is an execution service. We trade a proprietary trading firm evaluation that you have purchased and registered in your name, applying a risk plan built from the firm's published rules, and we hand over the funded account credentials once the firm verifies the pass.

The service consists of: reading and summarising the firm's rules; producing a risk plan; trading the evaluation; logging sessions and reporting progress; handling a reset if an attempt is breached; and handing over the funded account on verification.

3. What the service is not

To remove any ambiguity, the service is not, and does not include, any of the following.

  • Investment advice or a managed account service. We are not a broker, fund, investment adviser or portfolio manager, and we do not hold client money.
  • Trading of funded accounts. The engagement ends at handover. We do not trade funded accounts for a fee, a share of profit, or any other consideration.
  • Signals, education or mentorship. No trade calls, no community, no course, no ongoing subscription.
  • Any guarantee of funding. A prop firm decides whether an evaluation has been passed and whether an account is funded. We cannot overrule it.
  • Identity verification. KYC is conducted by the firm on its own platform, in your name.
  • Advice on the lawfulness of the arrangement in your jurisdiction. You are responsible for complying with the law where you live and with your firm's terms.

4. Fees and payment

The fee is a flat $220 per evaluation, regardless of firm, phase structure or account size, payable before trading begins. The prop firm's own challenge fee is separate, is paid by you directly to the firm, and is not included in our fee.

There is no subscription, no renewal, no performance fee, no profit split, no payout charge and no fee for retries covered by clause 6. Payment methods are confirmed at enquiry and depend on your location; the requirement is that the payment is traceable so both parties have a record.

Where a jurisdiction imposes a tax on the service, it is your responsibility unless the law requires us to collect it, in which case it is added and shown separately.

5. Your responsibilities

Five obligations sit with you, and the first is the most important.

  1. Check your firm's terms on third-party trading. Some firms prohibit it, some restrict account access, and some are silent. You must satisfy yourself that engaging this service does not breach your agreement with the firm. We will tell you what we know about a firm's policy, but the decision and the risk are yours.
  2. Register and pay the firm yourself. The account must be in your name, with your KYC, so that payouts are paid to you.
  3. Provide accurate information. The firm, plan type, account size and any known restrictions. A misstated plan is the most common cause of a plan built on the wrong rule set.
  4. Do not trade the evaluation yourself while the engagement is running. Two parties trading one account during an evaluation breaches firm rules, destroys the risk plan, and is a straightforward way to end the attempt.
  5. Keep the evaluation account funded and in good standing. If the firm suspends or closes the account for reasons unrelated to trading — a payment issue, for example — the evaluation cannot continue.

You also confirm that you are legally able to enter this agreement, that you are old enough to hold a prop firm account under your firm's terms, and that you are not resident in a jurisdiction subject to sanctions or a restriction that prevents participation.

6. Retries

If an evaluation is ended by a breach of a firm rule, we reset the attempt on the same firm and account size at no additional service fee. This is the practical meaning of "retries covered" and the reason a flat fee is charged rather than a per-attempt fee.

Three qualifications apply, and they exist because firms, not services, control most of the variables.

  • The firm's own reset or new-challenge fee is not covered by us. Many firms charge a reset fee, offer a free reset in a window, or require a new challenge purchase. That cost is the firm's and is outside our fee.
  • Retries apply to rule breaches, not to abandoned engagements. If you instruct us to stop mid-evaluation, or if you fail to keep the firm-side account active, the retry provision does not apply.
  • Structural changes require a new engagement. Moving to a different firm, a different account size or a different plan is a new assignment with its own fee, not a retry.

Where a firm changes its rules during an attempt in a way that makes the original plan invalid, the client is informed immediately and the options are set out: adapt the plan, pause, or stop. If a change ends the attempt, the session log is used to support a reset request with the firm, and our retry still applies.

7. Timelines

Timelines are quoted in trading days, in ranges, and are estimates rather than commitments. What determines the duration is the firm's rulebook: the profit target relative to the daily loss budget, whether the failure floor trails, the minimum trading days and the permitted instruments. We do not promise completion by a calendar date, because a firm can change terms, suspend a platform, or apply a rule on a definition different from its published one.

Where a firm imposes a window, the plan is built to finish inside it with margin rather than exactly at the limit. If the arithmetic does not support a requested deadline — a large target, a tight daily loss limit and a short window — the timeline is declined rather than attempted, because a rushed evaluation fails and consumes a retry that a properly paced attempt would not need.

8. Prop firm rules, compliance and the risk you carry

This clause deserves reading twice, because it is where the real risk of this arrangement sits.

Prop firms are independent businesses with their own terms, and those terms vary on the question of who may place trades on an account. Some firms state plainly that the account holder must trade personally. Others restrict the sharing of credentials or logins from multiple locations. Others say nothing specific and do not appear to enforce anything. Enforcement is frequently reactive rather than continuous: it tends to surface when something else draws attention to an account, such as a payout request or an unusual pattern.

What follows from that is unavoidable: an evaluation can be passed, an account funded, and the account later voided by the firm if its terms were breached. No passing service can eliminate that risk, and any service claiming to is misrepresenting the position.

Our approach is to be explicit rather than evasive. We tell you what we know about your firm's current terms. Where a firm's policy is a clear prohibition that is enforced in practice, we decline the assignment. Where the position is ambiguous, we say so, and the decision about whether to proceed is yours with the facts in front of you.

You also acknowledge that firm terms change without notice, that a policy in force at the time of purchase may not be in force later, and that any dispute about a firm's enforcement decision is between you and the firm. We will provide the session log and factual context to support you, but we cannot compel a firm to reverse a decision.

9. Funded accounts and payouts

A funded account is yours. We hold no ownership interest, no claim on profit, and no right to withdraw funds. Payouts are made by the firm to you under the firm's terms, which typically include a minimum number of trading days, a profit threshold, a consistency check and identity verification before the first withdrawal.

We do not trade funded accounts, do not supervise how they are traded, and do not provide trading signals at any stage. Questions about the mechanics of the funded phase — consistency calculations, payout timing, what triggers a review — are answered as part of the handover. Questions about which way a market is likely to move are outside the service.

You acknowledge that a funded account can be lost by trading it, that passing an evaluation is not a guarantee of eventual profit, and that no part of this service creates an entitlement to a specific payout.

10. No guarantee of results

We do not guarantee that any evaluation will be passed or that any account will be funded. Published performance figures — evaluations passed, completion rate, average time to pass — describe past engagements, exclude assignments we declined as unsuitable, and do not predict the outcome of yours. Market conditions, firm rule changes, platform outages and order handling are all outside our control.

11. Cancellation and refunds

Before trading starts, cancellation is straightforward: if you have paid and the evaluation has not begun, the fee is returned. Once trading has begun, the fee reflects work already performed, and the position is governed by the terms recorded in the engagement email — typically that no refund is available for work in progress, while the retry provision continues to apply if the attempt is breached.

The firm's challenge fee is never refundable by us, because it was never paid to us. Refund requests relating to a firm's own charge must go to the firm.

Where a fee was paid in error — a duplicate payment, or a payment for an engagement that was declined on compliance grounds — it is returned in full.

19. How these terms apply in practice: three scenarios

Clauses are abstract until they meet a real situation. Three common scenarios show where responsibility sits.

Scenario one: the firm voids a funded account two months after funding

An evaluation is passed, the funded account is handed over, and two months later the firm notifies the trader that the account is closed because its terms prohibit third-party trading. Under clause 8, this risk sits with the client: the terms were checked in advance, the ambiguity was flagged, and the firm's enforcement decision is the firm's own. What we provide is the session log and factual context to support an appeal, and no claim is made on the funded account either way. What we cannot do is compel the firm to reverse its decision, and no refund of the service fee is due in these circumstances — the work contracted for was performed. This is the worst outcome of the arrangement, it is disclosed before payment rather than after, and it is the reason the compliance check happens first.

Scenario two: an attempt is breached in week two

A futures evaluation is reset after a news-window restriction is applied more strictly than the firm's published schedule implied. Under clause 6, the attempt is reset on the same firm and size at no additional service fee, and the session log is used to support a reset request with the firm. If the firm charges its own reset fee, that cost is the firm's and not ours. If the same breach recurs on the reset attempt, the ruleset rather than the execution is treated as the problem, and the recommendation becomes a different firm — with the fee for the original engagement spent, and no pretence that it should be repeated.

Scenario three: the client changes their mind mid-evaluation

A client asks to stop in week two because they have decided to trade the evaluation themselves. Trading stops immediately, and the fee for work performed is not refunded under clause 11. The evaluation credentials are returned to the client's control, and the session log is retained for 24 months in case the firm asks questions about activity on the account. There is no penalty beyond the fee: the engagement simply ends, and the evaluation continues to belong to whoever holds the account.

The pattern: risks caused by the firm's own decisions sit with the client, work already performed is not refunded, and rule breaches that end an attempt are reset at no additional service fee. Everything else is a conversation.

20. Points that get disputed most often

Four points account for most disagreements, and stating our position here is fairer than arguing each one from scratch later.

"You said it would take two weeks." Timelines are quoted as ranges in trading days, not calendar days, because the rulebook sets the pace and calendar days include weekends and holidays that firms do not count. A quoted range of sixteen to twenty trading days is four to five calendar weeks once non-trading days are excluded, and that conversion is stated in the engagement email. Where a range has been exceeded, the log shows exactly which days were flat and why.

"The firm offered me a free reset, so why did I pay again?" You did not pay us again — the retry is covered under clause 6. If your firm charged its own reset fee, that is the firm's charge, and it is outside the service fee. Where a firm offers a free reset in a window, the attempt is restarted inside that window so the firm-side cost is nil.

"The account was funded but I cannot get a payout." Funded-stage conditions — minimum trading days, profit thresholds, consistency checks and KYC — are the firm's, and they are summarised at handover so they are known before trading the account. A payout refused for a consistency breach is a funded-phase rule, not an evaluation failure, and it is outside the scope of what was purchased.

"Can you keep trading it now that it is funded?" No, under clause 3. The answer does not change with the size of the account or the share of profit offered, because a service that earns from payouts has an incentive to influence when they are requested, and the account would be running under someone else's name.

Terms questions

Am I paying the prop firm's fee to you?

No. You pay the firm its challenge fee directly, and you pay ElitePropX $220 for the execution. The two are separate, and we never receive the firm's fee.

What is covered by a retry?

A rule breach that ends an attempt is reset on the same firm and account size at no additional service fee. The firm's own reset or new-challenge fee is not ours to cover.

Who owns the funded account?

You do, entirely. Registration and KYC are in your name, payouts are paid to you, and we hold no claim on the account or its profits.

Is a guarantee of passing available?

No, and it could not honestly be offered. Firm rule changes, platform outages and market gaps can end any attempt. What is offered is a structured process, session-level reporting and a covered retry.

What happens if my firm bans passing services?

We tell you before you pay if the firm's terms prohibit third-party trading and enforcement is credible, and we decline the assignment. Where the position is ambiguous, you decide with the facts stated.

Can I cancel after paying?

Before trading starts, yes, and the fee is returned. After trading starts, the fee reflects work in progress, though the retry provision still applies if the attempt is breached.

Do you take any share of payouts?

No. There is no profit split, no performance fee and no charge at withdrawal. The only fee is the flat $220 paid before trading begins.

What if I disagree with how a rule was read?

Raise it and the session log is attached to the reply. Where a firm's published rule was ambiguous, that is acknowledged — it is one reason retries are covered rather than billed.
