Privacy policy

This policy explains what information ElitePropX collects, why it is collected, how long it is kept, who else sees it, and what you can ask us to do with it. It describes what this website and this service actually do — not a template.

The short version

  • This site is a static website. It sets no cookies of its own and runs no advertising or analytics trackers.
  • We receive your email address when you contact us — by the enquiry form on the contact page or by email — and the evaluation credentials you provide if you engage the service.
  • We never ask for banking details or identity documents. KYC belongs to your prop firm.
  • You can ask for a copy of your data, or ask us to delete it, at any time.

1. Who is responsible for your data

ElitePropX ("we", "us") operates the website at elitepropx.com and provides a prop firm challenge-passing service. For the purposes of the UK/EU General Data Protection Regulation and comparable laws, ElitePropX is the data controller for the personal information described in this policy.

You can reach us about anything in this policy at support@elitepropx.com. This is also the address to use for data access and deletion requests, and for any complaint about how your information has been handled.

Two clarifications worth stating at the outset, because they determine what this policy does and does not cover. First, we are not a broker, a fund or a financial institution, so we hold no client money and no trading accounts of our own. Second, your prop firm is an independent controller of your data: it verifies your identity, holds your account and processes your payouts under its own privacy policy, which we do not control and cannot describe on its behalf.

3. Why we are allowed to hold it

Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, we rely on four legal bases depending on the purpose.

  • Contract. Most of what we hold — contact details, engagement details, evaluation credentials, progress records — is necessary to perform the service you have asked us to provide. Without it, the service cannot exist.
  • Legitimate interests. Correspondence records, payment records and the technical logs used to keep the site running and protected. We have a legitimate interest in maintaining an accurate record of what was agreed and in securing our systems, and this does not override your rights.
  • Consent. Where you voluntarily send additional information that is not needed for the service — for example, details about your trading history to inform a recommendation — we process it on the basis of your consent, and you can withdraw that at any time by asking us to delete it.
  • Legal obligation. Where we must keep a record of a transaction to comply with tax or accounting rules, we keep the minimum required for the period the law specifies.

4. What we use your information for

Five purposes, and nothing beyond them.

  1. Answering your enquiry. Including checking your firm's published rules and reporting any compliance caveat, which is why the firm name and account size are requested in the first reply.
  2. Performing the engagement. Building the rulebook summary and risk plan, trading the evaluation, and maintaining the session log.
  3. Handling resets and disputes. If an evaluation is ended by a rule interaction, the session log is the record used to support a reset request with the firm.
  4. Operating the website. Serving pages, keeping the site available, and preventing abuse such as scraping or denial-of-service traffic.
  5. Accounting and compliance. Keeping a record that a fee was paid, and cooperating with lawful requests where they arise.

We do not use your information for marketing unless you ask us to, and we do not send newsletters, promotions or follow-up sequences. There is no mailing list attached to this service.

5. Cookies and tracking on this website

This site is a set of static HTML, CSS, SVG and one small JavaScript file. It contains no analytics scripts, no advertising pixels, no social media trackers, no heat-mapping tools and no third-party fonts or CDNs. Because of that, the site sets no cookies of its own and no cookie banner is required.

The one third-party request the site can make is the enquiry form: when you press Send, the fields you filled in are transmitted over HTTPS to our form-processing service, which forwards them to our inbox. That request does not happen on page load, and the service sets no cookies on this site.

6. Who else sees your information

Personal information is disclosed to a short, specific list of parties, and only as far as each purpose requires.

  • Our hosting provider. The website is served by a third-party static hosting provider, which necessarily processes request logs and the IP addresses attached to them in order to deliver pages. The site itself stores no data on the host beyond the published files.
  • Email provider. Correspondence is handled through an email service, which stores the messages and metadata needed to deliver them.
  • Form-processing service. If you use the enquiry form rather than writing directly, the fields you submit — name, email, firm, account size, phase and message — pass through a third-party form-to-email provider before reaching our inbox. It processes the submission to deliver it and for spam filtering, and is not permitted to use it for anything else.
  • Payment processor. If you pay a fee, the payment is processed by a third party. We receive confirmation that a payment was made and a reference; we do not receive or store your card number or bank credentials.
  • Your prop firm. We interact with the firm's platform using the evaluation credentials you provide. The firm independently holds your identity data, account data and payout details under its own policy, and nothing we do changes how the firm handles them.
  • Professional advisers and authorities. Accountants, or a regulator or court, where there is a lawful requirement to disclose. This is rare, is limited to what is required, and you would be informed where the law permits.

We do not sell, rent, trade or share personal information for advertising, and no party on the list above is permitted to use your information for their own marketing in connection with our service.

7. International transfers

Our service is delivered remotely, and the parties listed above may process data in countries other than yours, including the United States and the European Union. Where a transfer leaves the UK or the European Economic Area and goes to a country without an adequacy decision, we rely on appropriate safeguards — typically the standard contractual clauses approved by the relevant authority — or on the necessity of the transfer to perform a contract with you.

In practice this matters most for email and payment processing, both of which are handled by established providers with published data protection terms. If you need to know the specific location where a particular item is processed before engaging, ask and it will be confirmed in writing.

9. How we protect it

Security measures are proportionate to what we hold, which is deliberately little: no identity documents, no banking details, no client funds. Practical controls include access limited to the individuals who need it, encryption in transit for the website and email, unique credentials for the accounts used in operations, and the practice of deleting evaluation credentials once an engagement closes.

One honest limitation: no service can guarantee absolute security, and email is not a confidential channel by design. Evaluation credentials sent by email exist in at least two mailboxes and in transit. Because of that, any information that would be damaging if disclosed — identity documents, bank credentials, passwords you use elsewhere — should not be sent to us, and is not needed for the service.

If a breach occurs that is likely to result in a risk to your rights, we will notify affected individuals without undue delay and, where required, the relevant supervisory authority.

10. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have some or all of the following rights. They can be exercised by emailing support@elitepropx.com, and no reason is required.

  • Access. A copy of the personal information we hold about you, in a portable format.
  • Rectification. Correction of anything inaccurate. Engagement details and correspondence are the items most often worth correcting.
  • Erasure. Deletion of information that is not subject to a legal retention obligation. Evaluation credentials and correspondence are usually deletable immediately; payment records are not, while tax law requires them.
  • Restriction. A hold on processing while a dispute about accuracy or lawfulness is resolved.
  • Objection. An objection to processing based on legitimate interests, which we will honour unless there are compelling grounds to continue.
  • Withdrawal of consent. Where processing is based on consent, withdrawing it stops that processing without affecting what was done before.
  • Complaint. The right to complain to your local data protection authority. We would rather hear from you first, but it is your right either way.

Requests are answered within one month. If a request is complex or you have made several, the period may be extended, and you will be told why. There is no charge for a reasonable request.

California and other US state residents

Where the CCPA/CPRA or a comparable state law applies, you have the right to know what personal information is collected and disclosed, to request deletion, to correct inaccurate information, and to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information. We do not sell or share personal information as those terms are defined, and we do not use or disclose sensitive personal information for purposes beyond providing the service. An authorised agent may make a request on your behalf with written authorisation.

14. What a typical engagement looks like from a data perspective

Privacy policies are easier to judge with a concrete sequence, so here is the full lifecycle of the information held about one client, from first email to deletion.

Day 1 — you send an enquiry. We hold your email address, the firm and account size you mention, and the contents of the message. If you describe previous failed attempts, that is stored too. Nothing is created about you beyond what you wrote.

Day 2 — we reply. The reply is stored alongside the enquiry. It contains the fee, a timeline range and compliance notes about your firm — a factual summary of the firm's published rules, not personal information about you.

Day 3 — you decide not to proceed. This is the common case, and nothing further is collected. Your enquiry and our reply sit in the mailbox for up to 12 months so that a later message has context, and are deleted sooner if you ask.

Alternatively, day 3 — you engage. The engagement adds the evaluation credentials you provide, the rulebook summary and risk plan generated for your account, and the session log produced while the evaluation runs. That is the complete set.

During the evaluation. The session log accumulates four data points per session: closing equity, distance from the failure floor, percentage of target completed, and trading days used. These describe the account, not you, and they are the record used if a reset or a dispute ever needs evidence.

At handover. The funded credentials are passed to you and the evaluation credentials are deleted within 30 days, because they have no further use. The engagement is over, and no ongoing service relationship exists.

Afterwards. Payment records are kept for the statutory period. The session log and correspondence are kept for up to 24 months in case a question arises, then deleted. If you ask for deletion sooner, everything except the payment record goes.

At no point in that sequence does any of the following exist: your identity documents, your home address, your bank details, your card number, a behavioural profile, or an advertising identifier. They are not collected, so they cannot leak.

15. Cookies and tracking on prop firm platforms

One source of confusion is worth addressing directly: prop firms' own websites and trading platforms are separate from this website, and they use tracking technologies extensively. A firm typically installs analytics, advertising pixels, affiliate attribution cookies and session recording tools on its own checkout pages, because it is tracking referrals and evaluating marketing spend.

When you visit a firm's site, its policies apply, and its cookie banner governs its own scripts. Those cookies cannot see this site, and this site's absence of cookies has no effect on them. If you want to understand what is tracked when you buy an evaluation, the firm's privacy policy and cookie notice are the documents to read — not this one.

The practical implication is straightforward: the privacy decision in this space is not whether a passing service collects data. It is whether the firm you are buying from does, and what its affiliate and analytics stack does with the click that led you there.

16. Making a request in practice

Rights are only useful if exercising them is straightforward. Ours are deliberately low-friction.

How to ask. Email support@elitepropx.com from the address you used to contact us, and state what you want: a copy of your data, a correction, or deletion. If you no longer have access to that address, say so and describe the engagement — the firm, the approximate date and the account size are usually enough to identify the record.

What happens next. You get an acknowledgement, then a substantive response within one month. Where a request covers several items with different retention rules, the reply itemises what was deleted, what was corrected and what was retained — including the reason for retention, which in practice means the payment record and the law that requires it.

What we will not do. We will not ask you to justify a request, charge for a reasonable one, or require you to use a form. We will also not tell a prop firm that you made a request, because the firm holds its own separate record and there is nothing to coordinate.

A note on timing around an engagement. Deleting the session log mid-evaluation removes the evidence that supports a reset request with the firm if a rule interaction ends the attempt. That is your choice to make, and the trade-off is stated here so the choice is informed rather than accidental.

17. Summary

This service holds a small amount of information for a short time: an email address, the details of an evaluation, credentials for an evaluation account, a session log, and a payment record. It sets no cookies, runs no trackers, sells nothing, and deletes credentials once the engagement ends. The information that would be most damaging if leaked — identity documents and bank details — is never collected, because the firm handles those and we have no use for them.

If anything in this policy is unclear, ask. A privacy policy that cannot be summarised in a paragraph is usually describing a business that collects more than it needs. Privacy questions

Do you need my identity documents?

No. Identity verification is a requirement of your prop firm, not of this service, and it happens on their platform. Sending documents to a passing service creates a risk with no benefit.

What credentials do you need?

The login for the evaluation account only. Funded account credentials are never requested, because we do not trade funded accounts.

Are evaluation credentials deleted afterwards?

Yes, within 30 days of the handover. They are of no further use once the funded account is in your possession.

Does this site use cookies?

No cookies of its own, and no analytics or advertising scripts. There is nothing to consent to because nothing is collected client-side.

Will I be added to a mailing list?

No. There is no newsletter or marketing list. You are contacted about your enquiry and, if you engage the service, about that engagement.

Do you share my details with prop firms?

We never pass your contact details to a firm for marketing. The firm already holds your account information as the account holder, under its own privacy policy.

Can you delete everything you hold about me?

Almost everything, on request. The exception is the payment record, which tax and accounting law requires us to keep for a defined period. That is stated in the retention section above rather than discovered later.

Do you use third-party analytics to measure traffic?

No. Aggregate traffic data comes from our host's request logs, not from a tracking script in your browser.
