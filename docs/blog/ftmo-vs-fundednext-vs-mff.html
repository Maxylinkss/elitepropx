FTMO vs FundedNext vs My Funded Futures: three rulebooks compared

FTMO and FundedNext are compared on the rows that decide an attempt: the loss floor, the daily stop, the billing rhythm and the payout conditions. They evaluate the same broad market, so the decision turns on mechanics and commercial terms. Numbers below describe each published rulebook's shape; confirm the current plan schedule before buying.

Key takeaways

  • FTMO: static, measured from the starting balance, one-off per attempt.
  • FundedNext: static on the two-phase model, trailing on some one-step variants, one-off per attempt.
  • One floor trails peak equity and the other is fixed, so the same equity curve can fail one and survive the other.
  • Daily stops: yes, equity-based versus yes, percentage of balance versus yes on most plans.

FTMO vs FundedNext: the two rulebooks side by side

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. FTMO uses a static, measured from the starting balance floor, yes, equity-based daily stop and one-off per attempt; FundedNext uses a static on the two-phase model, trailing on some one-step variants floor, yes, percentage of balance daily stop and one-off per attempt. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

Both firms evaluate forex and CFD accounts, so the instruments are broadly comparable and the decision turns on the rule mechanics and commercial terms. A trader who ignores this and compares only the headline target is comparing two different sports by their scoreboards.

Choose FTMO if your strategy fits a static loss floor, you can trade its session (weekday forex and CFD hours) and its billing rhythm (one-off per attempt) matches your expected timeline. It is specifically suited to traders who want the most conventional percentage-based evaluation available. The strongest reason to pick it is static maximum loss means progress never moves the failure point closer.

Choose FundedNext if your strategy fits a trailing loss floor, you can trade its session (weekday forex and CFD hours) and its billing rhythm (one-off per attempt) matches your timeline. It is specifically suited to forex traders who want to choose between a one-step and a two-phase structure. The strongest reason to pick it is several models let a trader pick between speed and a softer drawdown.

Where My Funded Futures fits in this comparison

My Funded Futures is the third product in this group and it changes the choice in a specific way: trailing on peak equity, monthly subscription and yes on most plans daily stop. It suits futures traders who want platform choice on a trailing evaluation. A three-way comparison is useful only if the third product changes the ranking of the first two; otherwise it adds a column without adding a decision.

The practical way to use the three columns is to eliminate rather than to rank. First remove any product whose market or session you cannot trade. Then remove any product whose loss-floor mechanic fights your holding period. Then compare billing against your expected timeline. The product that survives is the choice, and the other two were never candidates.

If all three survive, the decision is operational: support, dashboard detail, payout statement clarity and the reset policy. Those are the parts of the product you will use after the pass, and they are the parts a comparison table cannot score. Ask each firm a mechanics question in writing and judge the reply.
