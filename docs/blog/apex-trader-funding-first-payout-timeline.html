Apex Trader Funding First Payout Timeline

Apex is unusual among futures firms in two ways that shape the first payout: an evaluation can be passed in a single session because there is no minimum-day rule, and the funded account then requires five qualifying days, a safety net, a $500 minimum request and a 50% consistency test. This guide maps those gates in order, with the figures published in Apex's help centre on 21 October 2026.

Key takeaways

  • The evaluation has no minimum trading days. A pass is possible in one session, and the account expires after 30 days of access.
  • After passing, there are 7 calendar days to activate the Performance Account and pay the one-time activation fee.
  • The first payout needs 5 qualifying trading days, each meeting a minimum daily profit set by account size.
  • 50% consistency applies: no single day may account for 50% or more of profit since the last approved payout.
  • The safety net is the drawdown limit plus $100, and only profit above it can be requested. The minimum request is $500.
  • Each Performance Account is limited to six approved payouts, with a per-request maximum that rises by payout number.

Most first-payout questions are really one question: when does the money arrive. At Apex the answer is a chain of conditions rather than a single date, and each condition has its own clock. Some are measured in qualifying trading days, one in calendar days from the pass, and one in profit above a safety net.

Futures adds a variable that forex evaluations do not have: the drawdown threshold. On Apex it can be end-of-day or intraday depending on the evaluation bought, and the intraday version behaves differently on different platforms. Understanding that mechanism is the difference between a date that holds and a date that slips.

The order matters because the gates are not parallel. The qualifying-day count starts on the funded account, not at the evaluation, so a fast pass does not shorten it. The safety net is measured on the funded account, so profit made during the evaluation does not count toward the request. A trader who plans from the evaluation start will be wrong by the length of the funded phase.

The drawdown, in dollars

Apex sells two evaluation models, and the threshold behaves differently in each. The EOD model calculates the threshold once a day at the close from the ending balance and holds it fixed through the next session. The intraday model follows peak balance in real time, including unrealised profit, and never moves down.

AccountTargetDrawdownDaily loss limit (EOD only)

On a $50,000 intraday account the threshold starts at $48,000. If unrealised profit takes the balance to $50,900, the threshold moves to $48,900 immediately. On Rithmic and WealthCharts it stops rising once it reaches the $53,000 target balance, which happens when peak balance reaches $55,000. On Tradovate it keeps trailing the peak for the life of the evaluation.

In the Performance Account the intraday threshold stops at starting balance + $100 — $50,100 on a $50k account — once peak balance reaches starting balance + drawdown + $100, which is $52,100. That stop is the "safety net" in the payout rules, and it is why profit above it is what can be withdrawn.

From pass to Performance Account

When the target is reached and the rules have been respected, the evaluation is marked as passed and the trader has 7 calendar days to activate the Performance Account. The activation fee is one-time, not a subscription. Missing the 7-day window forfeits the pass.

The funded account is not a continuation of the evaluation. It is a new account with its own drawdown model, its own daily rules and its own payout conditions. A trader who passed on the EOD model receives an EOD Performance Account; one who passed on the intraday model receives an intraday Performance Account. The two have different payout tables, so the first decision after activation is to read the one that applies.

Two things can slow activation. The first is an account detail mismatch — a name, address or payment method that does not match the verification record. The second is leaving the activation until day six and discovering a payment problem. Both are avoidable by completing the details before the target is reached.

The five qualifying days

Apex requires at least five trading days before a payout, and a qualifying day is not simply a day with a trade. Each day must meet a minimum net profit for the account size: $100 on $25k, $200 on $50k, $250 on $100k and $300 on $150k on the intraday Performance Account. The days do not have to be consecutive.

That definition changes the plan. A trader who makes $1,500 in one session has one qualifying day, not five, and cannot request a payout until four more days each clear the minimum. A trader who makes $200 a day for five days has satisfied the count and is closer to a request than the first trader despite making less money.

The consistency rule usually resolves at the same time. No single profitable day may account for 50% or more of profit since the last approved payout. Five roughly equal days pass easily; one large day and four small ones do not, and the request option stays unavailable until ordinary days dilute the big one.

The safety net and the $500 minimum

The safety net is the drawdown limit plus $100, and it must be maintained for the life of the Performance Account. On a $50,000 intraday account that is $52,100. Only profit above the safety net can be requested, and the minimum request is $500 regardless of account size.

The "minimum balance to request" already combines the starting balance, the safety net and the $500 minimum. If the balance is below that figure, the payout option does not appear. A trader who withdraws down to the safety net on the first request will need to rebuild above it before the second.

Apex also caps each request and the number of payouts per account. On the intraday Performance Account the maximum is six approved payouts, and the per-request maximum rises with each payout — $1,500 on the first and $3,000 on the sixth for a $50k account. After the sixth, the account completes its cycle and a new evaluation is required.

The review and payment window

A payout request triggers a review of the trading record, rule compliance, identity, tax details and the payment method. Apex allows trading to continue while a request is under review, but the trader should treat the requested amount as already withdrawn: if the balance falls below the minimum threshold after submitting, the request is denied automatically.

  • Trading record. Whether trades were within session hours, within the position cap and consistent with the stated style.
  • Rule compliance. Whether the drawdown was ever breached, and whether any prohibited practice appears in the record.
  • Consistency. The 50% calculation over profit since the last approved payout.
  • Identity and tax details. Completed before the first request, not during it.
  • Payment method. The destination account must match the verified name.

A complete submission clears quickly. An incomplete one is held, and the hold does not announce itself — the request simply does not progress. Completing identity and payment details at activation removes the most common reason for a first-payout delay.

The evaluation took five days and the payout took another twelve. The gap is almost entirely funded-account time: two days to activate, five qualifying days, and a few days of administration. A trader who planned from the evaluation start would have expected cash around day eight and would have been wrong by eleven days.

The model assumes the threshold is reached without touching the drawdown and that the five days are roughly balanced. If one day dominates, the consistency test pushes the request back until ordinary days dilute it, which is the most common shape of a slipped first payout.

The third row is the one that surprises traders who passed comfortably. Because the 50% test is measured over profit since the last payout, a single large session can block the request even when the balance is well above the minimum. The fix is not a new account; it is more ordinary days.

Building the date backwards

  1. Start from the date you need the cash and subtract the settlement window in business days.
  2. Subtract the review window; add two days for a first request.
  3. Subtract the time needed to reach the minimum balance at your funded-account pace.
  4. Subtract five qualifying days, measured from activation.
  5. Subtract the activation window, then work back to the evaluation finish date.

Written as a chain, the date is usually further away than expected, and every step is a published condition rather than a delay. That is the useful output: a date the trader can plan around, and a clear view of which gate is binding. For most traders it is the qualifying-day count or the consistency rule, not the target.

Confirm every figure on the current help-centre page before relying on a date. Apex revised its consistency rule from 30% to 50% on current accounts, and older reviews still quote the legacy figure. A timeline built on last year's terms is a forecast of the wrong product.

Sizing the funded account

The size used to pass an evaluation is not always the size to use on the funded account. On Apex's intraday model the funded threshold stops at starting balance + $100, so once the safety net is reached the buffer grows with retained profit. Before that point, profit does not create room.

Account stageFloor behaviourAppropriate risk
Evaluation, EODRecalculated at the close, fixed during the sessionLargest risk that survives a normal streak
Evaluation, intradayFollows peak balance including unrealised profitReduce as the target approaches
Performance Account, before the stopFollows peak; buffer constantSmaller, because profit does not create room
Performance Account, after the stopFixed at starting balance + $100Can rise as buffer accumulates

A practical rule is to halve the risk at activation and restore it only after the threshold stops. That lengthens the approach to the payout minimum, but it converts the account from one that needs a perfect sequence into one that can absorb a bad week.

The alternative — keep the evaluation size and push for the minimum balance — produces the pattern most often reported on futures accounts: a fast first payout followed by a breach within weeks. The withdrawal reduced the buffer and the size never adjusted.

What the trailing stop changes

The stop is the point at which the intraday floor no longer follows equity. Before it, profit is a claim that can be taken back; after it, profit becomes room. On a $50k Performance Account the stop is $50,100 once peak balance reaches $52,100.

StateEffect of +$1,000 profitEffect of a $1,000 drawdown
Before the stopFloor rises; buffer unchangedBuffer unchanged until the floor stops
At the stopFloor fixed; buffer growsBuffer shrinks by the loss
Well above the stopBuffer grows one-for-oneBuffer shrinks, with room to spare

The first row explains why an account can be profitable and still fragile. A trader who is $2,000 up before the stop has not gained $2,000 of safety; they have gained paper profit and the same buffer as at the start. The safety net is what converts that profit into withdrawable room.

Compared with a forex payout

The timeline shape differs from a forex evaluation in three places, and each shortens or lengthens the wait.

StageApex futures timelineForex timeline
TargetDollar amount per account sizePercentage of the account
Floor after fundingTrailing until the stop, then fixedUsually static from the start
Payout gateFive qualifying days, safety net and consistencyCycle date plus profit share
First payout waitActivation, qualifying days, reviewSeasoning, cycle, review

The second row is the substantive difference. A forex funded account with a static floor can be traded at a constant risk because the distance to failure never changes. A futures account cannot, because the floor moves until it stops. That makes the futures risk plan a two-stage plan and the forex plan a single-stage one.

The payout review's evidence

Apex payouts are reviewed against a trade record, and the record is generated by the platform. A first request goes more smoothly when the trader can produce the same data the firm is looking at.

  • The full trade history export. Entry, exit, size, contract and session, from the first funded trade.
  • The daily equity curve. Shows the floor was never breached and where peak equity sat.
  • The qualifying-day list. Which sessions cleared the minimum daily profit, and by how much.
  • Identity and tax details. Completed at activation rather than at the request.
  • The payout method record. Matching the verified name and account.

The second item is specific to trailing-floor accounts. Because the floor follows peak balance on the intraday model, the relevant question is not the closing balance but the path: whether equity ever fell below the trailed level during a session. A trader who exports the equity curve has the answer; one who exports only closed trades does not.

The third item is a common source of avoidable delay. A count that looks satisfied on a dashboard may not be, because the plan requires a minimum net profit on each day, not merely a trade. Producing the session list and the profit per day side by side resolves the question before it becomes a hold.

Export the history monthly rather than at the request. Platform migrations and retention limits occasionally make older records harder to retrieve, and a local export is the only copy the trader controls.

Common questions

How long after passing does the first Apex payout arrive?

Allow two to three weeks. The pass must be activated within 7 calendar days, then five qualifying trading days are needed, then the balance must clear the safety net plus the $500 minimum, and the request goes through review. A fast, balanced funded account can finish sooner; a concentrated one waits on the 50% rule.

Does evaluation profit count toward the payout threshold?

No. The threshold is measured on the Performance Account, which is a separate account with its own drawdown and safety net. Profit made during the evaluation qualifies you for funding; it does not count toward the first withdrawal.

What is the safety net?

The drawdown limit plus $100. On a $50,000 intraday Performance Account it is $52,100. Only profit above it can be requested, and it must be maintained for the life of the account.

Why is my payout option unavailable after a good day?

Most likely the 50% consistency rule. If one profitable day accounts for 50% or more of profit since the last approved payout, the request option does not appear until ordinary days dilute it. The account remains active and trading can continue.

Can I request a payout before five qualifying days?

No. The count is a condition of eligibility, and each of the five days must meet the minimum daily profit for the account size — $200 on a $50k account, for example. The days do not have to be consecutive.

How many payouts can one Performance Account pay?

Six approved payouts, with a per-request maximum that rises by payout number. After the sixth, the account completes its cycle and a new evaluation is required.

Is Apex a monthly subscription?

No. A current evaluation is a one-time payment for 30 days of access, and the Performance Account activation fee is one-time. Legacy subscription accounts exist but are a different product.

Does an open position block a payout request?

Apex allows trading to continue after a request, but the requested amount is treated as already withdrawn. If the balance then falls below the minimum, the request is denied automatically. Size the account as though the cash had already left.
