Apex Trader Funding vs GOAT Funded Trader: which rulebook fits your trading

Apex Trader Funding and GOAT Funded Trader are compared on the rows that decide an attempt: the loss floor, the daily stop, the billing rhythm and the payout conditions. They evaluate different markets — futures versus forex and CFD — which changes the sessions, the sizing and the gap risk before any headline target is relevant. Numbers below describe each published rulebook's shape; confirm the current plan schedule before buying.

Key takeaways

  • Apex Trader Funding: trailing, measured from peak equity including open profit, monthly subscription per account.
  • GOAT Funded Trader: static and trailing variants, one-off per attempt.
  • Both floors use the same mechanic, so the decision turns on daily stops, billing and payout terms.
  • Daily stops: no separate daily stop on the standard evaluation versus yes.
  • A flat $220 service fee applies to any of the firms and any account size.

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. Apex Trader Funding uses a trailing, measured from peak equity including open profit floor, no separate daily stop on the standard evaluation daily stop and monthly subscription per account; GOAT Funded Trader uses a static and trailing variants floor, yes daily stop and one-off per attempt. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

Apex Trader Funding evaluates futures accounts while GOAT Funded Trader evaluates forex and CFD accounts. That difference is prior to every other comparison: the sessions, position sizing, costs and gap risk are not the same product category. A trader who ignores this and compares only the headline target is comparing two different sports by their scoreboards.

The table is a map of the questions to ask, not a verdict. Two firms can publish the same target and produce completely different pass rates because one measures loss from the starting balance and the other from peak equity. Before reading the rest of this comparison, identify which row would end your last attempt. That row is the one to compare most carefully.

Choose Apex Trader Funding if your strategy fits a trailing loss floor, you can trade its session (US futures session hours) and its billing rhythm (monthly subscription per account) matches your expected timeline. It is specifically suited to futures traders who can watch the US session and want a trailing-threshold challenge. The strongest reason to pick it is large account-size menu with a familiar subscription model.

Choose GOAT Funded Trader if your strategy fits a trailing loss floor, you can trade its session (weekday forex hours) and its billing rhythm (one-off per attempt) matches your timeline. It is specifically suited to forex traders who want a large headline balance at a low entry cost. The strongest reason to pick it is large account ladder.

Do not choose either on the basis of the profit split alone. A split is applied to profit that only exists if the account survives, and the account survives only if the rulebook matches the strategy. The split is the least informative number on the page, even though it is the one printed largest.

If both fit, choose on operational quality: support response, dashboard clarity, payout statement detail and the reset policy. Those are the parts of the product you will use every week after the pass, and they are the parts least visible before purchase. Ask a mechanics question before buying and judge the answer.
