How the Lucid Trading rulebook is built

The Lucid Trading evaluation is best understood as a set of constraints that interact, not as a headline profit target with some footnotes. The target decides how much you have to make. The loss floor decides how much room you have to make it. The daily stop, if there is one, decides how quickly a bad session ends. The minimum-day rule decides how long the attempt takes even when the target is reached early. Change any one of those and the same trading strategy produces a different outcome.

For Lucid Trading, the published structure is a evaluation with a trailing threshold. The loss floor is trailing, the daily stop is yes, and the consistency condition is yes. Billing is monthly subscription, payouts follow payouts after qualification, and the supported platforms are Tradovate, TradingView, NinjaTrader. Those are the rows that matter. Marketing pages tend to foreground the profit target and the profit split because both are easy to compare; neither tells you whether a given trade is survivable.

The trailing floor is the plan

Lucid Trading evaluates futures on a trailing threshold. The rule that ends most attempts is not the profit target but the high-water mark: a winning trade lifts the failure point, and a normal retracement can then close the account before the target is reached. Before buying, write down the starting threshold, the current equity high and the dollar distance between them. That distance, not the account size, is the risk budget for the session.

The correct size is a fraction of the remaining room. If the cushion is $2,000 and the plan is to risk $250 per trade, the account can absorb eight losses. If the cushion is $800, the same size allows three, which is too few for a normal distribution. Cut the size when the cushion shrinks and accept that the last third of the target is traded smaller than the first third.

A five-week schedule for a Lucid attempt

Week one is for measurement, not for a pass: take the same setups you would take on a demo, but record the remaining room before every entry. Week two is for calibration: compare intended risk with realised risk and cut any setup whose stop distance forces a size the plan cannot support. Week three is for the first push toward the target, with size fixed at the calibrated unit. Week four is for protecting the cushion after a gain, even if that means smaller positions. Week five is for the final push, with a hard rule that no new high is given back by more than half.

The schedule assumes the plan has no minimum-day requirement that forces activity. If it does, fill the required sessions with the smallest permitted size and treat them as administration, not as opportunities. A required session is not a reason to take a trade that the setup does not justify.

What to verify before paying

Confirm the exact trailing definition: does the threshold follow closed balance or peak equity including open profit? Confirm whether a daily stop applies, because a session stop changes the worst-case day. Confirm the payout qualification, including the number of qualifying days and whether a consistency test applies. Finally, confirm the platform and data-feed costs, because a monthly subscription plus a data fee is the real monthly cost of a slow attempt.

If any of those answers is unclear, ask support in writing before buying. A reply you can save is worth more than a comparison table written by someone who is not carrying the risk.

Common questions

Does Lucid Trading use a trailing drawdown?

Yes. The evaluation uses a trailing threshold, so the failure point follows the equity high. The room available for a retracement shrinks after a profitable run.

Does Lucid have a daily stop?

The plan includes a daily stop. Confirm the exact reset time and whether it is measured on balance or equity before relying on it.

How long should a Lucid attempt take?

There is no single answer, but a five-week schedule that separates measurement, calibration and the final push is more realistic than trying to pass in a week. Minimum-day rules and the trailing floor both shape the calendar.
