FundedNext vs Earn2Trade: the two rulebooks side by side

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. FundedNext uses a static on the two-phase model, trailing on some one-step variants floor, yes, percentage of balance daily stop and one-off per attempt; Earn2Trade uses a end-of-day trailing on most programmes floor, yes daily stop and one-off per programme. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

FundedNext evaluates forex and CFD accounts while Earn2Trade evaluates futures accounts. That difference is prior to every other comparison: the sessions, position sizing, costs and gap risk are not the same product category. A trader who ignores this and compares only the headline target is comparing two different sports by their scoreboards.

Forex menus against a futures programme

FundedNext evaluates forex and CFD accounts with a choice of one-step and two-phase models. Earn2Trade evaluates futures accounts through a programme that bundles structured education and uses an end-of-day trailing threshold on most offers. The market and session decide the platform and the hours; the trailing mechanic decides how size changes after a profitable close.

The education bundle is the second difference. Part of the Earn2Trade fee buys a course, which is useful for a beginner and redundant for an experienced trader. FundedNext sells the evaluation and the plan terms, with no curriculum attached. A trader comparing the sticker prices without accounting for the bundle is not comparing the same product.

How the floors change the plan

On a $100,000 FundedNext two-phase account with a static floor, a $500 risk unit has twenty losses of room if the floor is 10%. On a one-step variant with a 6% floor, the same unit has twelve. Earn2Trade's end-of-day trailing threshold behaves differently again: a $1,000 winner does not move the floor until the close, but it does move it after the close, so the next session starts with less room. A trader who sets size once and forgets it will gradually take more risk relative to the cushion.

The correct process is to recalculate the remaining room at the start of each session and divide by the planned risk unit. If the result is fewer than five losses, cut the size; if it is fewer than three, stop for the day. That rule works on any trailing account and is more useful than a fixed percentage table.

Billing differs: FundedNext charges once per attempt, Earn2Trade charges once per programme. Neither adds a monthly subscription on the offers compared here, so the calendar is not the main cost driver.

Which trader fits which

FundedNext fits a forex trader who wants to choose between a one-step and a two-phase structure and is willing to read the plan name. Earn2Trade fits a beginner who wants a futures evaluation with a course and an end-of-day trailing rule. An experienced futures trader may prefer a product that sells only the evaluation; an experienced forex trader may prefer the static two-phase option.

If you are new to both markets, the course is a real advantage, but it does not replace screen time. Budget for practice on a demo before the evaluation starts.

Common questions

Does Earn2Trade use an intraday trailing threshold?

Most programmes use an end-of-day trailing threshold, so open profit does not move the floor during the session. Confirm the exact programme before relying on that detail.

Are FundedNext plans all static?

No. The two-phase model is commonly static, while some one-step variants trail. The plan name is part of the rule.

Which firm includes a course?

Earn2Trade bundles structured futures education with its programme. FundedNext does not include a course in the evaluation.
