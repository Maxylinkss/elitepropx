FundedNext vs Earn2Trade: which rulebook fits your trading

These two firms are usually compared on price and target size, which are the two rows that matter least. FundedNext sells a forex and CFD evaluation with a menu of one-step and two-phase structures. Earn2Trade sells a futures programme that bundles a curriculum and measures the loss floor at the end of the day. The market, the sizing unit and the trailing mechanic change your behaviour long before either target becomes relevant. Figures below describe published rulebook shapes; confirm the current plan schedule before buying.

Key takeaways

  • The market row is prior to every other row: forex and CFD against futures decide the session, the platform and the sizing unit.
  • FundedNext's floor depends on the plan you pick. The two-phase model is commonly static; some one-step variants trail.
  • Earn2Trade measures its threshold at the close, so open profit does not move the floor mid-session, but the next session starts tighter.
  • Part of the Earn2Trade fee buys structured education. A trader comparing sticker prices without that bundle is comparing different products.
  • One-off billing on both sides means the real cost of an attempt is the fee multiplied by the number of retries.

The market row decides everything after it

FundedNext evaluates forex and CFD accounts. Earn2Trade evaluates futures accounts. That single row sets the trading hours, the data feed, the platform list, the contract specification and the way a loss is measured. A trader who compares the two on target size is comparing a percentage of a currency balance with a distance expressed in points on a futures chart. The numbers can be made to look similar; the work behind them is not the same.

The practical consequence is availability. Forex runs from Sunday evening to Friday evening with a daily rollover and no central exchange. Futures run on a defined session with a clearing house, a daily settlement and a maintenance window. A trader with a day job may only be able to watch the London or New York forex session; a futures programme may require presence during US hours. If the hours do not fit, the cheaper evaluation is the more expensive mistake.

There is also a capital-efficiency difference that rarely appears in comparison tables. Forex accounts are usually sized in notional currency with leverage applied to margin. Futures accounts are sized in contracts with a fixed margin per contract set by the broker. The same dollar risk produces a very different number of positions, and the position limit in each rulebook bites at a different point. Read the maximum contract and lot limits before you model a plan.

A menu of plans against a single programme

FundedNext sells a choice. The two-phase model runs a profit target in each phase and is the closest thing in the forex market to the classic evaluation. The one-step model collapses the process into a single target with a tighter floor. Because the plan name carries the rule, two FundedNext accounts bought on the same day can behave completely differently. A comparison that says "FundedNext uses a static floor" is only true of the model it happens to be describing.

Earn2Trade sells a programme. The path is an evaluation followed by a funded step, and the rule set is broadly consistent across the offers because the product is built around a single curriculum. That consistency is an advantage for a trader who wants to learn the rule once. It is a disadvantage for a trader who wants to optimise structure, because there is no one-step shortcut to choose.

The menu has a hidden administrative cost: the trader has to verify which rules attach to which plan, and support answers can lag a plan change. The single programme has a hidden cost too: if the structure does not fit your method, there is no alternative structure to move to. Neither model is better in the abstract; the question is whether you want to spend time choosing or spend time executing.

The education bundle and its real cost

Earn2Trade's programme includes structured futures education. That is unusual in this market, where most firms sell an evaluation and nothing else. For a trader who has never traded a futures contract, the curriculum has real value: it covers contract specifications, tick value, margin, session behaviour and the mechanics of a stop in a market that moves in fixed increments. For an experienced futures trader, the same material is a cost with no return.

The honest way to compare is to subtract the value you would place on the course from the fee before comparing it with FundedNext. If you would pay for a comparable course anyway, the Earn2Trade price is lower than it looks. If you would not, the bundle is dead weight and the comparison should be on the evaluation alone.

A second consideration is that education does not shorten an evaluation. The course teaches the instrument; it does not change the loss floor, the daily stop or the target. A trader who buys the programme expecting the curriculum to carry them through the challenge will still be measured on the same arithmetic as everyone else. Budget screen time on a demo separately, whether or not a course is included.

Floor arithmetic on a $100,000 account

Take a $100,000 FundedNext two-phase account with a static floor of roughly 10%. The failure point sits near $90,000 and stays there. A $500 risk unit has twenty losses of room, and a $1,000 winner does not change that room at all. That stability is the main attraction of the static model: the distance from the failure point is a constant, so position size can be set once and reviewed weekly.

Now take a one-step variant with a tighter floor, say 6%. The failure point starts near $94,000 and the same $500 unit has twelve losses of room. If the plan also trails, the floor follows equity upward and the room shrinks after every new high. Twelve becomes ten, then eight. A trader who never recalculates is gradually running a larger risk per trade relative to the cushion than the plan they thought they bought.

On the Earn2Trade side the threshold is a dollar amount rather than a percentage, measured against a peak that updates at the close. A $3,000 threshold on a $100,000 programme gives six losses at $500 of risk. After a $1,000 gain the floor moves up and the remaining room from the original balance falls to $2,000, which is four losses. Cutting the risk unit to $250 restores eight. The arithmetic is the same shape as a trailing forex floor; only the unit and the update timing differ.

End-of-day trailing versus intraday trailing

An intraday trailing floor moves whenever equity makes a new high, including on an open position. That means a trade that is $800 in profit and then reverses can still have raised the floor while it was open. The trader is punished twice: the winner becomes a loser, and the cushion shrinks as if the profit had been banked. This is the mechanic that catches out most traders who move from a static account to a trailing one.

An end-of-day trailing floor ignores open profit and updates at the close. The same $800 open position that reverses does not move the floor at all. That is materially friendlier to a swing-style approach that gives trades room. But it is not a free pass: at the close, the new peak is recorded, and the next session opens with less room. Traders who only look at the chart at the start of the day will not notice the floor has moved.

The correct habit on any trailing account is a two-minute pre-session check: current equity, current floor, remaining room, and the number of planned risk units that fit. Write the number down. When the number falls below five, cut size. When it falls below three, stop for the day. This single habit does more for survival on a trailing account than any entry filter.

The daily stop is a different number

A total loss floor says how much you may lose over the attempt. A daily stop says how much you may lose in one day. They are separate tests, and a breach of either ends the account. FundedNext applies a daily limit expressed as a percentage of balance on the plans compared here. Earn2Trade applies a daily loss limit in dollars on its programme. Both are checked against equity or balance depending on the firm, and that detail changes what counts.

If the daily stop is measured on equity, an open loss counts toward it immediately. If it is measured on balance, only closed trades count, which allows an open position to run through the limit without a breach until it is closed. Read which one applies before the first trade, because the two produce completely different behavior at the end of a session.

The daily stop also interacts with the trailing floor. On a trailing account, a bad morning can consume both the daily allowance and a slice of the total cushion. Two consecutive bad days can leave an account alive on paper but with too little room to trade its normal size. At that point the correct decision is to stop and reset the plan, not to trade smaller and hope. A shrink-to-fit approach on a trailing account usually ends with a slow bleed into the floor.

Consistency rules and plan names

Consistency rules cap how much of the total profit can come from a single day or a single trade. They exist to stop a trader passing on one oversized position, and they are the rule most often discovered late. FundedNext applies consistency conditions on some models and not others, which is another reason the plan name has to be read as part of the rulebook. Earn2Trade applies consistency conditions within its programme.

A consistency rule changes the shape of a good month. If no single day may exceed a set share of total profit, a trader who books a large day early is forced to either dilute it with more trading or wait for the rest of the month to catch up. The efficient response is to plan the distribution of profit before the month starts, not after the big day.

Ask support for the consistency threshold in writing, and ask whether it is measured on the evaluation, the funded stage, or both. The same firm can apply it in one stage and not the other. A comparison article cannot settle that for you, because the answer changes with the plan revision and the account type. The written reply is the only version that counts in a dispute.

Lots and pips against contracts and ticks

A forex position is sized in lots, with pip value depending on the pair and the account currency. A futures position is sized in contracts, with tick value fixed by the contract specification. The math is not transferable. A trader who risks 0.5% per trade on a forex account and then applies the same habit to a futures account will usually find that one contract is either far too large or far too small for the intended risk.

The correct approach is to build a table before the evaluation starts. For each instrument you intend to trade, write the value of one tick or one pip, the stop distance in ticks or pips, and the resulting risk at one unit. Then divide your risk budget by that number to get position size. On a $100,000 account with a $500 risk budget and a 20-tick stop on a contract worth $5 per tick, one contract risks $100, so five contracts fit. That is a calculation you can do once and reuse.

The position limit is the second constraint. Futures programmes often cap the number of contracts, and forex plans cap lots. If your normal size exceeds the cap, the plan is not a fit regardless of the target. Check the cap against your table before you buy, not after the first trade is rejected.

Platform stacks and what they imply

FundedNext runs on MT4, MT5 and cTrader. Earn2Trade runs on NinjaTrader, Tradovate and Rithmic-based front ends. The platform is not a neutral choice: it decides the order types available, whether stops are held server-side, how the DOM behaves in fast markets, and whether you can automate. A trader with an existing MT5 workflow has a real switching cost on the futures side, and a trader with a NinjaTrader workflow has the same problem in reverse.

Server-side stops matter more than most traders realise. If a stop lives only on the chart and the connection drops, the position is unprotected. Confirm that protective orders are held by the broker or the exchange, and test it on a demo by disconnecting deliberately. On futures, the exchange holds resting orders, which is a structural advantage. On forex, it depends on the broker's setup, and the firm is the broker.

Automation is the second differentiator. MT4 and MT5 support expert advisors, and cTrader supports cBots. NinjaTrader supports its own strategy framework and has a mature backtesting engine. If your method is rule-based, the platform you already know is worth more than a small difference in fee, because a broken automation costs a retry.

Sessions, settlement and overnight risk

Forex positions can be held through the week and are exposed to weekend gaps. A stop placed inside the gap will be filled at the first available price, which can be well beyond the level. That is why weekend holding rules matter and why many plans restrict or penalise positions held over the weekend. The risk is not theoretical: a Sunday open can erase a week of careful gains.

Futures positions face a daily settlement and a maintenance break. Holding through the settlement is normal, but the account is marked to market and the margin requirement is recalculated. A position that was comfortable in the morning can require more margin after the close. Traders who ignore the settlement schedule sometimes find themselves with a margin call that looks like a rule breach.

Both markets reward a session plan. On forex, decide in advance whether you will hold through the New York close and whether you will flatten before the weekend. On futures, decide whether you will trade the overnight session or only the regular hours, and size for the wider range. The rulebook may permit both; the trader's attention span usually does not.

How each payout is gated

FundedNext releases profit share on a cycle after the first payout, with the first withdrawal typically available after a seasoning period. Earn2Trade pays after qualification on its own schedule. The practical difference is the time between a good month and money in the account. A trader who needs cash flow should model the cycle, not just the split percentage.

The second gate is conduct review. Both firms can review trading for prohibited practices such as copying between accounts, latency arbitrage, or trading during restricted news windows. The review is where a technically valid pass becomes a refused payout. Keep the journal, keep the statements, and keep the support correspondence. A trader with records answers a review in an hour; a trader without them answers it over weeks.

The third gate is the payout method and the identity check. Withdrawals require verified identity and a matching payment method, and the first withdrawal is often the slowest because verification happens then. Complete verification before the first profitable month, not after. That way the payout request is a request rather than a process.

The cost of a failed attempt

Both firms bill once per attempt or programme on the offers compared here, so neither adds a monthly subscription that keeps charging while you are not trading. That makes the cost of failure easy to state: the fee multiplied by the number of attempts. A trader who fails twice on a $600 evaluation has spent $1,800 before funding. That number should be compared with the first payout, not with the headline account size.

Trailing floors raise the retry rate because they take room away after a good run. A trader who passes on a static account can fail on a trailing one with the same method and the same P&L curve, simply because the cushion shrank at the wrong moment. If you are choosing between a static and a trailing plan, the expected number of attempts is the variable to estimate, and it is usually higher on the trailing side.

The cheapest retry policy is not a discount code. It is a plan whose floor matches your drawdown distribution. Replay your last fifty trades against the floor and the daily stop. If the worst streak would have breached, the fee is not the problem; the plan is.

Which trader should choose which

FundedNext fits a forex trader who wants to choose between a one-step and a two-phase structure, values the MT4 and MT5 stack, and is willing to read the plan name as part of the rules. It also suits a trader who prefers a static floor and can verify which model carries it. The menu is the point: if you want to optimise structure, this is the side with something to optimise.

Earn2Trade fits a futures trader, particularly one who is new to contracts and would use the curriculum, and who prefers a single consistent rule set to a menu. The end-of-day trailing mechanic also suits a trader who holds positions through the session and does not want open profit to tighten the floor mid-trade.

A trader who cannot decide is usually optimising the wrong variable. The question is not which firm is better. It is which rulebook you can execute without changing your method. If neither fits, the correct answer is neither, and the fee stays in the account.

A checklist to run before checkout

Write down the plan name you are buying, the market it evaluates, and the floor type. Then confirm the daily stop basis, the consistency threshold, the minimum trading days, the maximum position size and the payout cycle. Six items, all of which appear in the terms. If support cannot answer one of them in writing, treat that item as unknown and assume the stricter reading.

Next, model the first ten trades. Take your normal risk unit, your normal stop distance and your normal win rate, and run the sequence against the floor and the daily stop. If the account survives the sequence with room to spare, the plan is a candidate. If it survives only because the sequence was kind, the plan is a coin flip.

Finally, price the whole path: fee, platform cost, data cost, and the expected number of retries. Compare that total with a realistic first payout, not with the account size. Most comparisons fail here, because the fee is visible and the retry cost is not.

Four comparison myths

"The higher target is the harder challenge." A target is only one constraint. A 10% target with a 10% static floor can be easier than an 8% target with a 5% trailing floor, because the floor decides how many attempts the method gets.

"A bigger account is always better." A bigger account with the same percentage floor gives more dollar room, which is useful only if your position size scales with it. Many traders keep the same lot size and simply take longer to hit the target.

"The cheaper firm is the better value." Value is the fee divided by the probability of a payout. A cheap evaluation with a floor that rejects your method has a low probability and a poor expected value.

"The rules are the same across plans." On a firm with a menu, the plan name is the rule. On a firm with a programme, the revision is the rule. Either way, the version you read at checkout is the version you are bound to, and it can change before your payout.

Common questions

Does Earn2Trade use an intraday trailing threshold?

Most programmes measure the threshold at the end of the day, so open profit does not move the floor during the session. The floor does move after the close, which tightens the next session. Confirm the exact programme before relying on the detail.

Are all FundedNext plans static?

No. The two-phase model is commonly static, while some one-step variants trail. The plan name is part of the rule, so read the terms for the specific model you are buying.

Which firm includes a course?

Earn2Trade bundles structured futures education with its programme. FundedNext sells the evaluation and its plan terms without a curriculum attached.

Can I trade forex on Earn2Trade?

No. Earn2Trade evaluates futures accounts on US session hours. FundedNext evaluates forex and CFD accounts on weekday forex hours.

Which is cheaper after retries?

It depends on how many attempts each floor forces. A trailing floor usually produces more retries than a static one, so the cheaper sticker price can cost more over a year. Model the expected number of attempts before deciding.
