FundedNext vs FTUK: which rulebook fits your trading

They evaluate different markets — forex and CFD versus futures — which changes the sessions, the sizing and the gap risk before any headline target is relevant. FundedNext vs FTUK: the two rulebooks side by side

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. FundedNext uses a static on the two-phase model, trailing on some one-step variants floor, yes, percentage of balance daily stop and one-off per attempt; FTUK uses a trailing on peak equity floor, yes on most plans daily stop and one-off per attempt. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

FundedNext evaluates forex and CFD accounts while FTUK evaluates futures accounts. That difference is prior to every other comparison: the sessions, position sizing, costs and gap risk are not the same product category. A trader who ignores this and compares only the headline target is comparing two different sports by their scoreboards.

Choose FTUK if your strategy fits a trailing loss floor, you can trade its session (US futures session hours) and its billing rhythm (one-off per attempt) matches your timeline. It is specifically suited to futures traders who prefer paying once instead of monthly. The strongest reason to pick it is one-off billing removes calendar pressure.

Forex and futures are not interchangeable

FundedNext evaluates forex and CFD accounts; FTUK evaluates futures. The first filter is therefore the market and the session, not the fee. FundedNext traders work weekday forex hours on MT4, MT5 or cTrader. FTUK traders work the US futures session on Tradovate, TradingView or NinjaTrader. A trader who cannot be at the screen during US hours should not buy the futures product, however attractive the one-off price looks.

The loss mechanics differ as well. FundedNext's two-phase model uses a static floor, while some one-step variants trail. FTUK uses a trailing threshold on peak equity. A static floor keeps the original dollar distance available; a trailing floor reduces it after every new high. That difference changes position size more than the headline target does.

A $100,000 account under each rule

Take a $100,000 account with a 10% static floor and a $3,000 trailing threshold. On the static account, a $500 risk unit has twenty losses of room for the whole attempt. On the trailing account, the same $500 unit has six losses of room at the start, and fewer after a gain. A trader who carries the forex size onto the futures account is taking more than three times the risk relative to the floor.

The correct futures size is derived from the remaining room. If the floor is $2,000 below current equity, a $250 risk unit gives eight losses, which is a reasonable buffer. The size must be recalculated after every new equity high, not set once at the start.

Both firms bill once per attempt on the plans compared here, so the entry fee does not grow with the calendar. That makes the trailing mechanic the main driver of retry cost: a tighter floor produces more failed attempts, and each attempt is a full fee.

The trader each product fits

FundedNext fits a forex trader who wants a static two-phase option, a familiar platform and a one-off fee. FTUK fits a futures trader who wants a one-off purchase instead of a monthly subscription and can trade the US session. A trader who wants both markets can use both, but should keep separate size tables and separate journals; the same strategy rarely transfers without adjustment.

If the decision is close, choose the market you can trade in your available hours. A rulebook you understand but cannot execute is worse than one you have to learn in a market that fits your day.

Is FTUK a forex firm?

Does FundedNext have a trailing floor?

The two-phase model is commonly static, while some one-step variants trail. Confirm the plan name and the floor definition on the plan page before buying.

Both charge once per attempt on the plans compared here, so the calendar does not add fees. The total cost depends on the number of retries, which the trailing floor can increase.
