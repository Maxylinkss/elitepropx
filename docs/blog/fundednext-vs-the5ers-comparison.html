FundedNext vs The5ers: which rulebook fits your trading

FundedNext and The5ers are compared on the rows that decide an attempt: the loss floor, the daily stop, the billing rhythm and the payout conditions. They evaluate the same broad market, so the decision turns on mechanics and commercial terms. Numbers below describe each published rulebook's shape; confirm the current plan schedule before buying.

FundedNext vs The5ers: the two rulebooks side by side

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. FundedNext uses a static on the two-phase model, trailing on some one-step variants floor, yes, percentage of balance daily stop and one-off per attempt; The5ers uses a static on most programmes floor, yes, programme-specific daily stop and one-off per programme. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

Two forex menus with different floor styles

FundedNext and The5ers both sell forex evaluations and both run a menu rather than a single plan. FundedNext's two-phase model is commonly static, while some one-step variants trail. The5ers uses a static floor on most programmes, with a menu that ranges from low-cost bootcamp-style entries to higher-stakes evaluations. The plan name is the rule at both firms, so a brand-level comparison is misleading.

The5ers' lower-balance programmes are the ones most often compared on price. A smaller account with a static floor can be a cheaper way to learn the mechanics, but fixed costs such as a VPS or data service take a larger share of the target. FundedNext's larger account menu gives more room for a strategy that needs a meaningful dollar risk unit.

A $25,000 and a $100,000 comparison

On a $25,000 account with a 10% static floor, the failure point is $22,500 and a $125 risk unit has twenty losses of room. On a $100,000 account with the same percentage floor, a $500 unit has the same twenty losses of room but a much larger dollar target. The percentage arithmetic is identical; the fixed costs are not. A $30 monthly data fee is 0.12% of the $25,000 account and 0.03% of the $100,000 account, which is why small accounts need a cheaper cost stack.

If the FundedNext one-step variant trails, the size calculation changes. A $500 gain raises the floor and reduces the room from the original balance. The trader then has to cut size or accept a smaller buffer. On The5ers' static programme, the original room stays available, which is simpler for a trader who wants a fixed risk unit.

Both firms bill once per attempt or programme, so the entry fee does not grow with the calendar. The number of attempts is still the main cost variable.

Which menu fits

FundedNext fits a forex trader who wants a choice between one-step and two-phase structures and a larger account ladder. The5ers fits a trader who wants a static floor, a low-cost first programme and a straightforward MT5 setup. A trader who needs the largest possible account should compare the top of each ladder; a trader learning the mechanics should compare the smallest.

If a plan is described as trailing, treat it as a different product from a static plan, even if the brand and the target are the same. The floor definition is the row that decides how the account is traded.

Common questions

Are all The5ers programmes static?

Most programmes use a static floor, but the menu is large and the programme name matters. Confirm the floor definition on the specific programme page.

Does FundedNext offer a low-balance entry?

It offers several account sizes, but the exact ladder changes by model and cohort. Compare the plan page rather than a third-party list.

Which firm is better for a first evaluation?

The low-cost The5ers programmes are often chosen for a first attempt because the entry price and static floor are easier to learn on. FundedNext is a better fit once the trader knows which model they want.
