Apex Trader Funding vs Bulenox: which rulebook fits your trading

Apex Trader Funding and Bulenox are compared on the rows that decide an attempt: the loss floor, the daily stop, the billing rhythm and the payout conditions. They evaluate the same broad market, so the decision turns on mechanics and commercial terms. Numbers below describe each published rulebook's shape; confirm the current plan schedule before buying.

Key takeaways

  • Bulenox: trailing, one-off or subscription depending on promotion.
  • Daily stops: no separate daily stop on the standard evaluation versus yes on some plans.

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. Apex Trader Funding uses a trailing, measured from peak equity including open profit floor, no separate daily stop on the standard evaluation daily stop and monthly subscription per account; Bulenox uses a trailing floor, yes on some plans daily stop and one-off or subscription depending on promotion. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

Both firms evaluate futures accounts, so the instruments are broadly comparable and the decision turns on the rule mechanics and commercial terms. A trader who ignores this and compares only the headline target is comparing two different sports by their scoreboards.

Choose Bulenox if your strategy fits a trailing loss floor, you can trade its session (US futures session hours) and its billing rhythm (one-off or subscription depending on promotion) matches your timeline. It is specifically suited to cost-sensitive futures traders who will read the plan terms carefully. The strongest reason to pick it is frequent promotions lower the entry cost.

Trailing floors, different billing pressure

Apex and Bulenox both evaluate futures on a threshold that follows peak equity, so the row that decides an attempt is not the headline target but the moment the floor starts moving. At Apex the threshold includes open profit, which means an unrealised winner raises the failure point before the trade is closed. Bulenox uses a trailing threshold as well, but the attached rule set changes with the promotional plan, so the first thing to confirm is which plan generation the checkout page is selling.

The billing difference is larger than it looks. Apex charges a monthly subscription per account, so a pass that takes ten weeks pays for roughly three months. Bulenox sells some plans as a one-off and others as a subscription depending on the promotion. Two traders can compare the same sticker price and end up with different total costs simply because one bought during a promotion and the other did not.

A $50,000 futures attempt, line by line

On a $50,000 account with a $2,500 trailing threshold, the failure point starts at $47,500. A $1,000 winning trade lifts the floor to $48,500 once the profit is counted, so the room below the new high is still $2,500 but the room from the original balance is only $1,500. A normal retracement that gives back $1,600 now ends the account even though the trader is still profitable on the day.

That arithmetic is why size has to fall after a good run. A trader risking $250 per trade has ten losses of room at the start; after a $1,000 gain the same $250 risk has six losses of room from the new floor. Cutting to $150 restores a reasonable buffer. Bulenox's trailing rule behaves the same way; the only reliable difference is the plan terms attached to the promotion, which can include a daily stop on some offers and not on others.

Add the subscription cost before comparing the two. At Apex, three months of billing is part of the cost of a slow pass. At Bulenox, a one-off promotion can be cheaper for a slow pass and more expensive if the trader needs a reset. Neither structure is universally cheaper; the expected number of attempts decides.

Which futures trader belongs on which platform

Apex suits a trader who wants a large account ladder, a familiar monthly subscription and a rulebook that is widely documented. Bulenox suits a cost-sensitive trader who is willing to read each promotion's terms and accept that the plan attached to a discount may not be the standard one. A trader who needs a daily stop should verify it plan by plan at Bulenox rather than assuming it is included.

Platform access is nearly identical, so it should not decide the choice. Both firms work with Tradovate, Rithmic and NinjaTrader, and both run on US futures session hours. The real decision is whether the trader can live with a floor that moves and a billing rhythm that charges for calendar time. A trader who needs a fixed failure point should be looking at a static forex evaluation instead of either of these futures products.

Common questions

Does Apex include open profit in its trailing threshold?

Yes. The threshold is measured from peak equity including open profit, so an unrealised gain can raise the failure point before the trade is closed. A retracement after that can end the account even if the closed balance never reached the new high.

Is Bulenox always cheaper than Apex?

No. Bulenox runs frequent promotions, and some plans are one-off while others are subscriptions. A promotional price can be cheaper for one attempt, but the attached rules and reset terms decide the total cost across retries.

Which firm has a daily stop?

Bulenox includes a daily stop on some plans; Apex does not apply a separate daily stop on its standard evaluation. Confirm the exact plan before buying, because both firms sell more than one generation of terms.
