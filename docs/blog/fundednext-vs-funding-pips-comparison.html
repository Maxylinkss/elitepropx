FundedNext vs Funding Pips: which rulebook fits your trading

FundedNext and Funding Pips are compared on the rows that decide an attempt: the loss floor, the daily stop, the billing rhythm and the payout conditions. They evaluate the same broad market, so the decision turns on mechanics and commercial terms. Numbers below describe each published rulebook's shape; confirm the current plan schedule before buying.

Key takeaways

  • Funding Pips: static, measured from the initial balance, one-off per attempt.

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. FundedNext uses a static on the two-phase model, trailing on some one-step variants floor, yes, percentage of balance daily stop and one-off per attempt; Funding Pips uses a static, measured from the initial balance floor, yes, percentage based daily stop and one-off per attempt. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

Read the plan name before the brand

FundedNext and Funding Pips both sell forex evaluations with one-off billing, and both publish a two-phase structure that looks similar at the top of the page. The difference is inside the plan menu. Funding Pips describes a two-phase evaluation with a static loss floor measured from the initial balance. FundedNext sells one-step and two-phase models, and some one-step variants use a trailing floor. Two FundedNext traders can therefore be trading different loss mechanics under the same brand.

The practical check is to open the plan page, find the words static or trailing, and confirm whether the floor is measured from the starting balance or from peak equity. If the page says trailing, model the account as one where a winning trade reduces the room available for the next trade. If it says static, the original dollar distance stays available for the whole attempt.

Where the one-step model changes the arithmetic

A one-step evaluation removes a phase but usually compresses the loss allowance. On a $100,000 account with a 6% floor instead of a 10% floor, the failure point is $94,000 rather than $90,000. A $500 risk unit has twelve losses of room instead of twenty, and a trailing variant reduces that room further after a gain. The shorter path is real, but it is bought with a smaller margin for error.

Funding Pips' two-phase static structure gives the trader the full floor for both phases, which is easier to size against but adds a second target and a longer calendar. A trader with a steady method may prefer the slower route because it does not require a size reduction after every new high. A trader with a short window may prefer the one-step model and accept the tighter floor.

Both firms bill once per attempt, so the fee does not grow with the calendar. The cost of a slow pass is the time itself, not another month of subscription. That makes the expected number of attempts the main cost variable, and the tighter floor of a one-step model can increase that number.

The operational differences that remain

Platform access is similar, with MT4, MT5 and cTrader available at both firms. Payouts are released on a fixed cycle after the first withdrawal at both, and the consistency condition varies by plan at FundedNext while Funding Pips usually applies none on the evaluation. A trader who relies on a few large days should confirm the payout condition before choosing the cheaper plan.

If the two are close on price, choose the plan whose floor you can describe from memory. A static floor is easier to hold in your head during a session; a trailing floor requires you to track the high-water mark. The rule you can follow under pressure is worth more than a slightly lower fee.

Common questions

Does FundedNext use a static or trailing floor?

It depends on the model. The two-phase model is commonly described as static, while some one-step variants use a trailing floor. The plan page, not the brand page, is the definition.

Is the Funding Pips evaluation always static?

The two-phase product is described as a static floor measured from the initial balance. Confirm the exact plan at checkout, because firms can change terms between cohorts.

Which is better for a small account?

The one-step model can be cheaper in time, but its tighter floor leaves less room for a normal losing streak. A small account should usually prioritize the floor size over the speed of the path.
