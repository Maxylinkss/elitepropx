Prop firm rules are a small vocabulary with large consequences. The same word can mean different things at different firms, so the definition in the terms page is the only one that binds. The consequence is always numerical. A rule changes the dollar value of a stop, the number of trades a day can absorb, or the amount that reaches a payout. Reading it as a number rather than a slogan is what makes it usable.

What counts as account sharing

Account sharing usually means allowing another person to access the trading account or to make trading decisions for it. The rule can cover a login from another device, a family member placing a trade, a signal group sending entries, or a service trading the account. The common thread is control: if someone other than the account holder can affect the account, the firm may treat it as sharing.

The rule is not limited to a password. A trader who shares screen access, gives a VPS login to a third party or follows a signal service that executes on the account may be inside the definition even if the account holder presses the button. Read the clause for the words access, control and instruction, not just the word sharing.

Why firms prohibit it

The firm's risk model assumes one trader making decisions on one account. Sharing breaks that assumption: it can multiply the same position across accounts, hide the true risk owner, or allow a strategy that the firm did not agree to. It also creates an identity problem at payout, because the firm has to pay the person who owns the account, not the person who traded it.

The consequence is usually severe: the account can be closed, the payout can be refused, and the fee may be lost. Some firms offer a written permission process for a specific arrangement, such as a passing service, but the permission has to be explicit and usually covers the evaluation only, not the funded account.

How to stay on the right side of the rule

Keep the login to yourself. If a service trades the evaluation, confirm in writing that the firm permits third-party trading and that the permission covers the work being done. Do not use a shared VPS or a device that another trader can access. Do not copy trades between accounts unless the firm explicitly allows it. Keep a record of every access method and every permission.

If the firm asks about a login from a new location, answer honestly and provide the explanation before the account is flagged. A clear answer with evidence is easier to resolve than a pattern of unexplained access. The rule is strict because the risk is real, not because the firm wants to make administration difficult.

Common questions

Can a family member trade my account?

Usually not without explicit permission. The rule is about control, not just the password. A family member placing trades can be treated as account sharing.

Is a passing service allowed to trade my evaluation?

Only if the firm terms permit third-party trading and the permission covers the work. Get the clause in writing before paying the service.

What happens if I share an account?

The account can be closed and the payout refused, even if the trading was profitable. The fee may not be refunded.
