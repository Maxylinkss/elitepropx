What the prop firm passing EA is

An expert advisor is a program that runs inside MetaTrader and places, manages and closes trades on a chart without anyone clicking. Most EAs sold online are general-purpose: a trend follower, a grid, a scalper. This one is not. It is a prop firm passing EA, written around the constraints a challenge imposes, and its only objective is to move an evaluation from its starting balance to its profit target while staying inside the daily loss limit, the maximum loss limit, the minimum trading days and any consistency rule.

The difference is not marketing. A general EA optimises for profit per unit of time and treats a drawdown as an inconvenience. A passing EA treats a drawdown as the thing that ends the product. It will stop trading for a day, trade smaller near the target, and take ordinary small wins instead of the one big position a general strategy would prefer — because the evaluation does not pay for the biggest day, it pays for a finished attempt.

In practice it is one file attached to one chart. It reads the licence, detects the account size, applies a risk profile sized from that balance and works through its session windows. There is no signal group to follow and no screen to watch; it can run on a VPS overnight.

  • Balance-aware sizing. Risk per trade comes from the account's daily loss limit, not from a fixed lot size, so a $25,000 demo and a $200,000 evaluation run the same logic at different numbers.
  • A hard daily stop. When the session's loss budget is spent, the EA stops opening trades until the next session. It does not "recover" the day.
  • A target lock. Once the target is reached, trading stops and the account sits still — the phase where self-managed attempts most often give the pass back.
  • Day-count pacing. Where a firm needs a minimum number of trading days, activity is spread across the calendar instead of sprinting to target in three sessions.
  • Consistency awareness. Where a firm caps any single day's share of profit, daily profit is capped so the record clears at payout, not only at the target.
  • News and weekend handling. Positions are avoided around restricted releases and flattened before the weekend where the terms require it.

What it is not: a guarantee, a funded account, or a substitute for checking whether your firm permits automation at all.

This is the whole reason the page exists. Nobody should buy trading software on the strength of a sales page — ours included. Step one: open a demo with a good broker

"Good" means three practical things, and none of them involve affiliate commission — there is no referral link on this page.

  1. The broker offers MT4 or MT5. The EA runs inside MetaTrader, so the demo has to be a MetaTrader demo. Nearly every forex and CFD broker provides one in about two minutes.

Futures traders should say so up front. Most prop-firm futures accounts run on Tradovate, Rithmic or NinjaTrader rather than MetaTrader, so the test is set up differently — usually a NinjaTrader demo or a simulated evaluation on the same feed.

Step three: the EA trades the demo to the target

The EA is attached, the profile loads, and it starts working under the same rule set a challenge would impose:

What the test proves, and what it cannot

A free test is only worth something if both sides are clear about what it measures. A demo is a genuine forward test of the EA's behaviour; it is not a prop firm evaluation.

What you can judge

  • Whether it reaches the target in the sessions it claimed, on a live forward feed.
  • How it sizes — constant risk derived from the balance, or drifting with confidence.
  • How it behaves on a losing day, including whether the daily stop is respected.
  • Whether the drawdown is boring. A passing EA should produce a dull curve. Excitement is a defect.
  • Whether the reporting agrees with the history you can read yourself.

What it cannot prove

  • That your firm's feed matches. Prop firm spreads and slippage differ from a broker demo.
  • That your firm permits automation. That is a terms question, answered by the clause, not by performance.
  • The funded phase. Passing and keeping an account are different jobs, and the demo tests neither payout rules nor consistency at withdrawal.
  • Future results. Two weeks is a sample of process, not a forecast.

Use the demo as a filter. If the fortnight looks like a rule set being executed, buying the licence is a reasonable next step. If trades look improvised or the answers to your questions are vague, the test has done its job by costing you a fortnight instead of $570.

How it trades an evaluation

There is no secret indicator, and any vendor who says otherwise is selling a story. What makes a passing EA work is the order of decisions: rules first, risk budget second, market view last and least important.

1. It reads the balance and derives the budget

On attach, the EA computes the daily loss budget from the firm's limit. On a $100,000 account with a 5% daily limit that is $5,000, and risk per trade is a fraction of it — a third at most — so three consecutive losses leave the session survivable. Nothing else in the program may override that number. This is what separates a passing EA from a merely profitable one: a strategy can be profitable on average and still fail a challenge, because the challenge does not average.

2. It waits for its session windows

It trades only inside defined hours, chosen for liquidity. Outside them it does nothing, which is most of the day. Restricted releases are excluded, and where the terms require flat positions over the weekend it closes before the cutoff rather than hoping the gap goes its way. Every hour not traded is an hour in which the budget cannot be spent.

3. It opens small, defined-risk positions

Each entry carries a stop from the moment it is placed, sized so a full stop equals the planned risk. No averaging down, no adding to a loser, no grid and no martingale — the four techniques that produce spectacular screenshots and dead evaluations. Position count is capped too, because three correlated positions on one currency complex is one position with three times the risk.

4. It paces towards the target

The target becomes a daily pace using the sessions available and the firm's minimum day count. If the required pace would eat too much of the daily budget, the EA extends the calendar instead of increasing size. A pass that takes twelve days is worth infinitely more than one that reaches 90% of target and breaches a daily limit on the last session.

5. It locks the target and stops

The moment the target is met, trading stops. This is not caution for its own sake: a large share of failed attempts are accounts that passed the target and kept trading while waiting for verification, giving back enough to breach a rule the evaluation had already satisfied.

6. It respects the consistency rule where one applies

Firms that cap any one day's share of total profit create a failure that happens after the target — the account passes and the payout is withheld. The EA caps daily profit during the build and continues working ordinary days until the distribution clears the threshold. Where no consistency rule applies, this module simply does not bind, which is why the firm has to be named before the profile is loaded.

The four rules it manages for you

Across every firm we deploy on, the same four rules end attempts. The EA does not make them disappear; it makes them mechanical, which is the difference between knowing a rule and obeying it at 3 a.m. on a losing day.

RuleHow it usually failsWhat the EA does instead
Daily loss limit One oversized position, or losses recovered with double size Risk fixed at a fraction of the budget; trading stops when it is spent
Maximum loss / trailing floor A floor that follows equity upward and is hit after a good week Size recalculated against the floor as it moves, keeping set distance
Consistency rule One huge day that passes the target and fails the payout check Daily profit capped during the build, extra ordinary days added
Minimum trading days A three-day sprint, then a wait for eligibility Activity spread so target and day count land together

The trailing floor. A firm whose maximum loss follows peak equity is not measuring your balance, it is measuring your best day. Every dollar of profit raises the line beneath you, so risking the same percentage after a strong week risks more in absolute terms against a closer floor. That is how traders blow up from a winning position. The EA recomputes the distance on every trade and shrinks size as the floor climbs.

The consistency rule. This one is invisible until payout. An account can reach a 10% target with a single 7% day, get verified, get funded, and then have the first withdrawal refused because that day is 70% of the profit. The deliverable is not a passed evaluation — it is a pass that still works when you ask for the money.

Firms, platforms and the permission check

Platforms. MT4 and MT5 cover most forex and CFD prop firms: FTMO, FundedNext, The5ers, FXIFY, FundingPips, Alpha Capital and their competitors. The EA ships for both. If your firm only offers cTrader, DXtrade or Match-Trader with no MetaTrader, the EA cannot run there, and that is the end of the conversation rather than something to work around.

Futures firms. Apex, Topstep and MyFundedFutures run on Tradovate, Rithmic, NinjaTrader and TradingView. The same rule logic is applied through a NinjaTrader strategy build where the firm and platform support automated execution. Ask before assuming your plan is covered.

The permission check. This matters more than any performance number, and it is free. Before the EA goes on a paid evaluation we read the automation clause on your specific plan and tell you which of three things is true:

  • Permitted. The plan allows automated execution and does not prohibit the strategy type. The EA runs at the price on this page with nothing extra to pay.
  • Permitted with conditions. Some plans allow EAs but restrict high-frequency behaviour, require disclosure, or disable automation above a certain size. The EA is configured inside the condition or the size is changed — never the clause.
  • Prohibited. We say so in writing and do not sell you the licence for that firm. A funded account can be voided at payout for something the evaluation never tested.

The distinction the market gets wrong is between "EAs allowed" and "this strategy allowed". Many firms permit automation and prohibit behaviour some EAs produce — hundreds of orders a minute to harvest a spread, latency games, hedging across accounts. This EA trades a handful of defined-risk setups a session, which sits inside the permission at every firm that permits automation at all. That is a claim to verify against your clause, not a licence to skip it.

Read this before paying. Some firms prohibit automated trading outright, and enforcement usually surfaces at the payout rather than at the pass. We would rather lose the $570 than sell a licence that gets your funded account closed.

How fast a pass actually is

On a two-phase forex challenge with percentage targets, the binding constraint is almost never the profit target — it is the calendar. A phase that requires four trading days cannot be completed in two, however much the account earns. The EA is configured so the target and the day count land on the same date, which puts the realistic first-attempt window at eight to fourteen days on most plans, and shorter where there is no minimum day requirement.

Single-phase and instant-funding products move faster because there is less calendar to satisfy. Futures plans with dollar targets can be quicker still, though their trailing thresholds punish acceleration, so the EA paces them rather than rushing them.

Three things genuinely slow a pass down. Minimum trading days cannot be compressed: if the plan needs ten, the pass takes at least ten. Restricted news windows shorten the usable calendar in a week with several releases, and the EA sits out rather than trading through. A losing week triggers a pause that costs sessions and protects the account — days, not the attempt.

Set-up, VPS and install

The technical side is small, but it has to be right, because an EA that stops running mid-session is worse than no EA at all.

  1. A MetaTrader terminal on Windows. The EA attaches to a single chart and needs no templates or indicators.
  2. AutoTrading enabled. The toolbar button must be green and "Allow automated trading" ticked in the options. This one setting causes most "it is not trading" messages.
  3. A VPS. It keeps the terminal running when your laptop sleeps or the connection drops. Any low-latency Windows VPS near the broker's server works, costs a few dollars a month, and prevents a gap in the equity curve.
  4. The right account. The EA goes on the evaluation account, not the demo, with the profile written for that firm and balance.
  5. A log you keep. Export the trade history at the end of each session. If a firm ever questions an account, the record is the answer.

Installation takes about fifteen minutes. Support covers the attach, the settings and the common errors; it does not cover changing the strategy.

After the pass: the funded phase

A pass is not the finish line. The funded account has its own rules — a profit threshold, minimum trading days per payout cycle, a consistency check and verification — and three things change the moment the EA stops:

  • Change the password. You hold the funded account alone, which is both safer and more defensible.
  • Re-read the funded terms. Evaluation rules and payout rules are different documents, and the differences are where first payouts get refused.
  • Decide whether to keep using the EA at a smaller risk profile. Many clients do; some trade manually once the pressure is gone. What is not legitimate is trading the funded account with no written plan.

Risks and honest limits

This page has made strong claims about the EA, and they are meant. It is also software, and software that trades carries risk no sales page should blur.

  • A pass is not a payout. Firms can change rules or refuse a withdrawal for reasons unrelated to how the account was traded.
  • Firm permission is yours to confirm. We tell you what the clause says, but the account is in your name and the consequences land there.
  • Market conditions change. The daily stop and the floor limit damage; they do not eliminate it.
  • Execution differs between servers. Expect small differences in fills, and size for them.
  • The licence is software, not a service. The done-for-you route includes retries and a human watching the account. The EA includes neither, which is why it is priced once rather than per evaluation.
  • No income promise. Any vendor who guarantees what a funded account will earn is describing a scenario rather than a product.

None of that is a reason to avoid the EA. It is the reason the free test exists.
