Order the rules by what ends the account

Every rulebook has three tiers. The first tier ends the account immediately: the maximum loss, the daily loss and any prohibited conduct. The second tier blocks the payout: consistency conditions, minimum trading days and verification requirements. The third tier changes the economics: platform fees, payout cycles and reset prices. A trader who treats all rules as equally important spends attention on the third tier and breaches the first.

Write the first tier on a card before the first trade: the dollar value of the total floor, the dollar value of the daily stop and the list of conduct that is prohibited. Those are the numbers and behaviours that cannot be recovered from. The second tier belongs in the calendar, and the third tier belongs in the budget.

The pre-trade check that prevents most breaches

Before each entry, confirm four things: the remaining room to the floor, the loss already used in the session, the size implied by the stop distance, and whether a scheduled event restricts the trade. If any of the four is unknown, the trade is not ready. This check takes less than a minute and catches the two most common breaches: a position that is too large for the remaining room and a trade opened inside a news window.

The check also creates a record. A journal entry with those four numbers is enough to reconstruct why a trade was taken and whether the risk was within the plan. If a breach happens, the record shows whether it was a sizing error, a rule misunderstanding or an operational failure. That distinction matters when asking for a reset or a review.

What to do after a breach

Stop trading the account immediately. Confirm whether the breach is hard (the account is closed) or soft (a warning or a payout restriction). Read the reset policy before paying for a new attempt. If the breach was caused by a platform or data error, collect the evidence before contacting support: order tickets, screenshots with timestamps and the account statement. A claim without evidence is a complaint; a claim with evidence is a review request.

If the breach was a sizing error, the next attempt should change the process, not just the intention. A smaller risk unit, a hard daily stop and a pre-trade check are process changes. "I will be more careful" is not.

Common questions

What is the most common rule breach?

Position size relative to the remaining room, followed by trading inside a restricted news window. Both are preventable with a one-minute pre-trade check.

Can a breach be reversed?

Some firms offer a reset or a review if the breach was caused by a platform error. A genuine rule breach is usually final. Collect the evidence before contacting support.

Should I pay for a reset after a breach?

Read the reset price and the new terms first. A reset is only worth it if the cause of the breach has been fixed in the process, not just in the intention.
