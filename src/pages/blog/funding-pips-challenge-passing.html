How the Funding Pips rulebook is built

The Funding Pips evaluation is best understood as a set of constraints that interact, not as a headline profit target with some footnotes. The target decides how much you have to make. The loss floor decides how much room you have to make it. The daily stop, if there is one, decides how quickly a bad session ends. The minimum-day rule decides how long the attempt takes even when the target is reached early. Change any one of those and the same trading strategy produces a different outcome.

For Funding Pips, the published structure is a two-phase evaluation with a static loss floor. The loss floor is static, measured from the initial balance, the daily stop is yes, percentage based, and the consistency condition is usually none on the evaluation. Billing is one-off per attempt, payouts follow profit split on a fixed cycle, and the supported platforms are MT4, MT5, cTrader. Those are the rows that matter. Marketing pages tend to foreground the profit target and the profit split because both are easy to compare; neither tells you whether a given trade is survivable.

The static floor is only half the plan

Funding Pips runs a two-phase forex evaluation with a static floor measured from the initial balance and a percentage daily stop. The static floor means the failure point does not move when the account earns, so a fixed risk unit remains valid across both phases. The daily stop is the rule that ends a bad session, and it is the number most traders under-plan for.

On a $100,000 account with a 10% floor and a 5% daily stop, the failure point is $90,000 and the session limit is $5,000. At a $500 risk unit, the account can absorb twenty losses in total but only ten in one day. A trader who takes ten losses in a day has used the entire daily allowance and cannot recover within the session; stopping earlier is the only sensible plan.

A two-phase calendar that respects the daily stop

Phase one should be treated as a measurement phase. Take the setups your journal already supports, size at the calibrated unit, and stop at three losses or the daily stop, whichever comes first. The goal is to reach the target without ever touching the daily limit, not to reach it quickly. Phase two repeats the same process with the same size; a static floor does not require a size reduction after a gain, so the plan can stay stable.

If the plan has a minimum-day requirement, use the required sessions to observe and record rather than to force trades. A session with no valid setup is a successful session if it preserves the floor. The consistency condition, if one applies at payout, is easier to satisfy when the profit is built from a series of normal days.

What to verify about the firm and the service

Before paying for an evaluation, confirm the daily stop reset time and whether it is measured on balance or equity. Confirm the maximum loss definition and whether it includes open profit. Confirm the payout cycle, minimum withdrawal and any consistency rule. If a passing service is involved, confirm that the firm permits third-party trading and that permission covers the funded account as well as the evaluation. A service that cannot produce that clause in writing is not a safe route.

Finally, confirm the platform: MT4, MT5 and cTrader are listed, but the account type should match the strategy's execution needs. A plan that fits on paper can still fail on a platform that cannot place the orders the method requires.
