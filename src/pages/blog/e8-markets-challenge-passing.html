How to pass an E8 Markets challenge: the rulebook, the math and the plan

E8 Markets no longer sells a two-phase evaluation. Its 2026 line-up — One, Pro, Signature and Zero — is built on single-phase products with different targets, drawdown mechanisms and payout rules. That makes "which E8 product" the first decision, not "which phase", because a 6% target on a dynamic floor and an 8% target on a static floor with a daily profit cap are different challenges. This guide uses the figures published in E8's help centre and product pages on 21 October 2026.

Key takeaways

  • All current E8 products are single-phase. There is no two-phase E8 evaluation to choose in 2026.
  • E8 One: 6% target, 3% daily drawdown, 4% dynamic drawdown, 40% best-day rule at payout.
  • E8 Pro: 8% target, 2.5% daily drawdown, 8% static drawdown, 2% daily profit cap, no consistency rule.
  • E8 Signature: 6% target, EOD dynamic drawdown, 35% best-day rule, 80% split and a payout buffer.
  • There is no minimum trading-day count on One or Signature, but at least one trade every 60 days is required.
  • The daily rule differs by product: a hard drawdown on One and Pro, a soft pause on Signature's performance account.

The E8 rulebook in one page

E8 sells forex and CFD products on Classic Markets and separate futures products, with the market selected at checkout. The market choice changes leverage and the instrument list, not the drawdown or payout mechanics. Every product is single-phase, and the differences between them are the target, the drawdown mechanism, the daily rule and the payout gate.

The word that does most of the work in any E8 description is the product name. One, Pro and Signature share a 6–8% target band but differ on almost everything else: One has a 4% dynamic drawdown, Pro an 8% static drawdown, Signature an EOD dynamic drawdown with a payout buffer. A trader who compares "E8" to another brand without naming the product is comparing a logo to a rulebook.

One, Pro or Signature

One is the product for a trader who wants the lowest target and accepts a tighter floor. A 6% target against a 4% dynamic drawdown leaves a target-to-floor ratio of 1.5:1, which is demanding: the account has to earn half again its risk budget before it passes. The 3% daily drawdown adds a session constraint that binds before the floor on a bad day.

Pro is the opposite trade. An 8% target against an 8% static floor is a 1:1 ratio, and the floor never moves. The cost is a 2.5% daily drawdown and a 2% daily profit cap, which limits how fast the target can be reached. A trader who makes 2% in a morning has finished for the day whether they want to or not.

Signature sits between them. The target is 6%, the drawdown is EOD dynamic rather than intraday, and the payout gate is a 35% best-day rule with a buffer equal to the drawdown and a minimum of five profitable days between payouts after the first. It is the cheapest of the three, and the payout conditions are the most detailed.

The practical choice is driven by failure mode. A trader who gives back intraday profit should avoid One's dynamic drawdown. A trader who makes one large day a month should avoid Signature's 35% rule. A trader who trades small and often can use any of the three and should choose on fee.

Dynamic against static arithmetic

Assume a $100,000 account on Pro: an 8% static floor at $92,000 and an 8% target at $108,000. Room is $8,000; target is $8,000. The ratio is 1:1, and the room does not change as the account earns, so the risk unit can stay constant for the whole attempt.

Now assume One on the same size: a 4% dynamic floor at $96,000, a 6% target at $106,000. Room is $4,000; target is $6,000. The ratio is 1.5:1, and the floor rises with the highest closed balance until it locks at $100,000. A trader who closes $2,000 of profit lifts the floor to $98,000, leaving the same $4,000 of room from a $102,000 balance. Profit does not create safety until the floor locks.

Signature's EOD dynamic drawdown behaves the same way but is calculated once a day on the end-of-day balance, so intraday equity swings do not move it. That is more forgiving than One for a trader who holds through retracements, and less forgiving than Pro for a trader who wants a fixed unit.

The static floor also means the room grows in relative terms as the account earns. On Pro, after $4,000 of profit the balance is $104,000 and the floor is still $92,000, so the buffer is $12,000 rather than $8,000. A trader who keeps the original unit is taking less relative risk, which is a rare case where doing nothing is the correct adjustment.

Planning a One attempt

A 6% target on $100,000 is $6,000. With a $250 unit and a 2:1 payoff, a winner returns $500, so the target is twelve net winners. At two trades per session and a 45% hit rate, twelve net winners takes roughly forty-five trades, or twenty-three sessions — about five weeks.

The drawdown budget should be half the 4% floor, not all of it. A trader who uses $4,000 of room to reach a $6,000 target has taken on more risk than the target implies, and the expected number of attempts rises accordingly. Half the floor keeps the risk per dollar of target at a workable level.

The unit should be held constant until the floor locks. A dynamic drawdown rewards a steady sample and punishes a size increase after a good week, because the floor has already risen. If the method cannot reach the target at a constant unit in five weeks, the answer is a longer calendar, not a larger unit.

The close is compressed because there is only one phase. Reduce to two thirds of the unit at 70% of the target and a third at 90%. The final sessions are for protecting the result and preparing documentation, not for pressing toward a number already within reach.

Planning a Pro attempt

An 8% target on $100,000 is $8,000, against an $8,000 static floor. With a $300 unit and a 2:1 payoff, a winner returns $600, so the target is fourteen net winners. The 2% daily profit cap is $2,000, which limits a day to about three net winners; a trader cannot compress the target into two sessions.

That cap is the defining constraint. It converts the attempt into a minimum of four profitable days even if the method produces one ideal setup, and it makes the everyday distribution look like the funded record. A trader who dislikes caps should choose One or Signature instead.

The 2.5% daily drawdown is $2,500, which at a $300 unit is eight losses. A personal cap of three losses ($900) keeps the session well inside both the daily rule and the static floor, and leaves the floor intact for a bad week.

Because the floor does not move, the plan can be paced rather than compressed. A trader who finishes in four weeks and one who finishes in ten pay the same fee, and the slower attempt has more room to absorb a losing streak. On a static product, patience is free.

Planning a Signature attempt

Signature's 6% target is $6,000 on $100,000, with an EOD dynamic drawdown of 3% ($3,000) that locks at the initial balance. The challenge has no daily breach rule; the 2% daily pause applies to the performance account, where it stops trading for the day without closing the account.

That makes the challenge more forgiving than One on the daily side but tighter on the floor: $3,000 of room against a $6,000 target is a 2:1 ratio, the most demanding of the three. A trader needs a small unit and a longer calendar, or a higher hit rate.

The payout conditions are where Signature differs most. A 35% best-day rule applies to total profit, the minimum payout is $100, and after the first payout at least five profitable days (each 0.3% or more) are required between requests. A buffer equal to the EOD drawdown must remain in the account and cannot be requested. On futures accounts the maximum is four payouts per account.

Those conditions reward a trader who produces a steady distribution and penalise one who relies on a single large session. If the method is lumpy, Signature is the wrong product regardless of its lower fee.

Lot sizing and pip value

Forex sizing is pips × pip value × lots. On a standard lot of a USD-quoted major, one pip is about $10, so a 20-pip stop risks $200 per lot and a $250 unit buys one lot with a little room. A 40-pip stop buys half a lot. The position count is a consequence of the stop distance, not a decision in itself.

Pip value varies by pair and by account currency. USD/JPY depends on the exchange rate, crosses run through two conversions, and metals and indices do not use pips in the same way at all. A trader who applies $10 per pip universally will mis-size the non-USD-quoted instruments, and the error is largest when volatility is highest.

The buffer is the difference between a planned unit and a realised one. Spreads widen around the London open and around releases, and stops slip. Adding ten to twenty percent to the planned risk changes the lot count by a fraction and keeps the realised loss inside the intended unit.

Leverage is the last check and rarely the binding one on Classic Markets: forex is 1:30, indices and metals 1:15, crypto 1:1. A correctly sized position on a $100,000 account uses a small fraction of available margin. The only way leverage binds is when the size was wrong to begin with.

The daily rule as a session budget

E8's daily rules are not uniform. One applies a 3% hard daily drawdown, Pro 2.5%, and Signature applies no daily breach in the challenge but a 2% soft pause in performance. A trader comparing products on the target alone will miss the rule that ends most bad weeks.

The basis decides how the limit behaves. Measured on equity, floating losses count immediately, so an open position $1,500 underwater on a $100,000 One account has used half the 3% allowance before it is closed. Confirm the basis; the difference is thousands of dollars on one trade.

When the clock resets is the last part of the definition, and it is not the same as the session hours. A trade opened late in one session and closed after the roll may land in either day's tally, which matters for a method that holds past the New York close. If the document does not say, flatten before the roll.

The daily rule should also be paired with a weekly cap. Four sessions at a personal limit of three losses is twelve losses, which can consume half a 4% floor. A weekly cap of one third of the floor stops the accumulation before it becomes structural.

Consistency, by product

This is the row most often generalised wrongly. E8 One applies a 40% best-day rule at payout, Signature 35%, and Pro states that it has none. A trader who reads one product page and assumes the rest will either over-constrain a Pro attempt or under-constrain a Signature one.

The test to run before choosing a plan is the share of profit from the best day. Take sixty trading days, total the profit, and compute that share. If it is above a third, the distribution is lumpy and a Signature payout will bind. If it is above 40%, One will bind too. Pro is the product for a method that cannot dilute its best day.

The design response for a lumpy method is a daily profit cap and a trade count large enough to dilute the best session. That flattens the distribution without reducing the total. The second response is to choose the product whose payout rule matches the method's natural shape.

Platforms and market choice

E8 separates Classic Markets from futures at checkout, and the choice changes leverage, instruments and, on some products, the payout mechanics. Classic Markets runs forex at 1:30, indices and metals at 1:15 and crypto at 1:1. Futures accounts have their own contract limits — for example 2 contracts on a $25,000 Signature account and 12 on a $150,000 one.

The platform follows the market. Classic Markets is a MetaTrader 5 environment; futures accounts run on a separate stack with their own contract specifications. A trader with an MT5 expert advisor cannot assume it runs on the futures product, and a futures trader cannot assume the same order types carry across.

Automation raises a rulebook question separate from the platform. E8 permits copy trading across accounts that belong to the trader, but commercial or shared automation is a different matter. Read the activity clauses, test on a demo under the same rules, and keep the logs.

Session and pair selection

Forex liquidity is concentrated in London and New York, and the overlap between them is the deepest window of the day. A method should be built for one window rather than for the whole day, because a London breakout and a New York reversal have different stop distributions, trade frequencies and daily loss profiles.

The pair carries as much information as the hour. EUR/USD and GBP/USD are liquid in London; USD/JPY is active in Tokyo and New York; AUD and NZD pairs move in the Asian session. A method that works on EUR/USD at 09:00 does not transfer to AUD/USD at 02:00 without re-measuring the spread and the stop distribution.

The daily rule interacts with the session because volatile sessions consume it faster. A trader whose method trades the London open will reach the personal cap sooner than one who trades the quiet afternoon, with the same unit. That is a reason to size the session against the cap, not a reason to avoid volatility.

A ten-week calendar

Weeks one and two are measurement: half the unit, a full record of setups, and a note of spread and slip on every entry. The output is a table of how the method behaves under the account's conditions, including which sessions and pairs are expensive. No target work happens here.

Weeks three to seven are the working block: full unit, a fixed setup list, a personal daily cap of three losses, and a weekly cap of one third of the floor. The target should be within reach by week seven. If it is not, reduce the unit or extend the calendar rather than increasing activity.

Weeks eight and nine are the distribution block: two thirds of the unit, and a deliberate effort to spread the remaining target across sessions. On Signature this is where the five profitable days between payouts start to accumulate; on One it is where the 40% best-day share falls.

Week ten is the close: a third of the unit, the target protected, and the payout documentation prepared. Ten weeks is a realistic calendar on a product with no time limit, and it costs no more than a four-week sprint that fails.

Risk of ruin and the longest losing run

Assume a 45% hit rate and a 2:1 payoff. Expectancy is 0.45 × 2R − 0.55 × 1R, or 0.35R per trade, which is healthy. The probability of six consecutive losses is 0.55 to the sixth, about 2.8%. Across fifty trades the chance of seeing at least one such run is high, so a plan that cannot survive six losses fails in most attempts rather than in unlucky ones.

Size so that the longest run from your own record, plus two, consumes less than half the floor. On a $3,000 Signature floor that is a $250 unit for a six-loss tolerance. On One's $4,000 floor it is about $330. A smaller unit produces a longer calendar, which is a fair trade on a product with no time limit.

The daily rule changes the shape of the calculation by interrupting runs. A cap at three losses converts a long streak into a sequence of short ones across sessions, and the account continues with the floor intact. That is a genuine benefit of a daily rule, and it is why the personal cap matters more than the firm's generous limit.

Releases, spreads and restricted windows

Forex news risk is concentrated in central bank decisions, inflation prints and employment data, and the reaction can be tens of pips in seconds. Spreads widen before the release, liquidity thins, and the stop that was 20 pips away at 13:55 is not the stop that fills at 14:30.

E8's products differ on news. One allows news trading in the challenge but prohibits it in the performance account around high-impact releases, beginning five minutes before and ending five minutes after. Signature states that news trading is allowed without restrictions on both stages. Pro publishes no news restriction on its product page. Those are three different rulebooks under one brand.

The cautious approach is to close before the release and wait for the first hour to pass before re-entering. It gives up some of the largest moves and removes a category of dispute. Anyone who holds should cut the position and size the stop for a gap rather than for normal volatility.

From pass to payout

The split and the gate are the two numbers that decide what a pass is worth. One and Pro allow up to 100% depending on the product and the option chosen at purchase; Signature pays 80%. Pro processes payouts daily with a 1% minimum profit, One requires the payout to exceed 50% of the daily drawdown, and Signature requires a buffer equal to the EOD drawdown plus five profitable days between payouts.

Those mechanics matter more than the headline split. A 100% split with a buffer requirement and a five-day count is not the same product as an 80% split with none. The comparison should be the net amount available on the first request, not the percentage advertised.

The funded review examines the record: size relative to the rules, distribution of profit, number of sessions and any pattern resembling prohibited practice. A complete journal answers the questions in an afternoon; a statement without one invites interpretation.

Which plan fits which trader

One suits a trader who wants the lowest target and can trade a tight unit on a dynamic floor. It is the fastest of the three in calendar terms and the least forgiving on a give-back.

Pro suits a trader who wants a static floor, a fixed unit and no consistency rule, and who can live with a 2% daily profit cap. It is the most predictable product of the three and the easiest to plan around.

Signature suits a trader who wants an EOD drawdown, a lower fee and can produce a steady distribution that satisfies a 35% best-day rule and five profitable days between payouts. A trader whose profit arrives in one large day a month should not buy it.

Across all three, the same discipline applies: a fixed setup list, a personal daily cap, a weekly cap at one third of the floor, and a unit that survives the longest losing run from the trader's own record.

Common questions

Does E8 Markets offer two-phase evaluations?

Not in its current line-up. One, Pro, Signature and Zero are single-phase products. Older reviews describing an E8 two-phase 8%/5% evaluation are describing a discontinued product.

Which E8 product has a static drawdown?

E8 Pro, with an 8% static floor and no consistency rule. One uses a 4% dynamic drawdown and Signature an EOD dynamic drawdown, both of which lock at the initial balance.

What is the 2% daily profit cap?

On E8 Pro the account can make at most 2% in a day. It limits how quickly the 8% target can be reached and makes a multi-day distribution unavoidable, which is why Pro can advertise no consistency rule.

How many payouts can a Signature account pay?

Four on futures accounts purchased after 1 October 2026, with a buffer equal to the EOD drawdown left in the account and at least five profitable days between payouts after the first. Confirm the cap for the Classic Markets account separately, because the payout mechanics differ by market.

Can I trade news on E8?

It depends on the product. One prohibits news trading in the performance account within five minutes either side of a high-impact release. Signature allows it without restriction. Check the product page for the account being bought.
