How the E8 Markets rulebook is built

The E8 Markets evaluation is best understood as a set of constraints that interact, not as a headline profit target with some footnotes. The target decides how much you have to make. The loss floor decides how much room you have to make it. The daily stop, if there is one, decides how quickly a bad session ends. The minimum-day rule decides how long the attempt takes even when the target is reached early. Change any one of those and the same trading strategy produces a different outcome.

For E8 Markets, the published structure is a one-step and two-phase evaluations. The loss floor is static on most plans, the daily stop is yes, percentage based, and the consistency condition is often none on the evaluation. Billing is one-off per attempt, payouts follow profit split on a fixed schedule, and the supported platforms are MT4, MT5, cTrader, Match-Trader. Those are the rows that matter. Marketing pages tend to foreground the profit target and the profit split because both are easy to compare; neither tells you whether a given trade is survivable.

One-step and two-phase plans are different products

E8 Markets sells one-step and two-phase forex evaluations, with a static floor on most plans and a percentage daily stop. The two structures are not the same product with a shorter queue. A one-step plan removes a phase but usually compresses the loss allowance; a two-phase plan adds a second target but gives more room. Before comparing prices, decide which structure your method can actually trade.

The static floor makes the sizing simpler than a trailing product: the dollar distance to failure stays fixed, so a 0.5% risk unit remains 0.5% of the starting balance for the whole attempt. The daily stop is the variable that changes the worst-case day. On a 5% daily stop, a $100,000 account can lose $5,000 in a session; at a $500 risk unit, that is ten losses. The plan should never require the account to survive more than the daily stop in one session.

Building a pass around the daily stop

Start with the daily stop, not the target. If the daily stop is $5,000 and the normal risk unit is $500, the session can absorb ten losses, which is more than a normal distribution needs. A trader who takes five losses in a day should stop regardless of the remaining allowance, because the next five losses would consume the entire session budget and leave no room for a recovery. A three-loss rule is often a better personal limit than the plan's maximum.

Then map the target. A 10% target on a $100,000 account needs $10,000. At a $500 unit and a 2:1 plan, a winning trade contributes $1,000, so ten net winners are needed. If the plan has a minimum-day requirement, spread those winners across the required sessions rather than trying to finish early. The calendar is part of the risk plan, not an obstacle to it.

Platform and payout checks

E8 lists MT4, MT5, cTrader and Match-Trader. If you use Match-Trader, confirm that the plan supports it; if you use MT5, confirm that the account type matches your expert advisor or indicator setup. On the payout side, confirm the cycle, the minimum withdrawal and whether a consistency condition applies at the funded stage. A one-off fee makes a slow pass cheaper than a subscription, but it does not make a refused payout acceptable.

Read the one-step and two-phase terms side by side. The plan name is the rule; the brand is only the dashboard.
