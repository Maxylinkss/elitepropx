FTMO vs Trade The Pool: which rulebook fits your trading

FTMO and Trade The Pool are compared on the rows that decide an attempt: the loss floor, the daily stop, the billing rhythm and the payout conditions. They evaluate different markets — forex and CFD versus equities — which changes the sessions, the sizing and the gap risk before any headline target is relevant. Numbers below describe each published rulebook's shape; confirm the current plan schedule before buying.

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. FTMO uses a static, measured from the starting balance floor, yes, equity-based daily stop and one-off per attempt; Trade The Pool uses a firm-specific, typically a hard loss limit floor, yes daily stop and monthly subscription. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

FTMO evaluates forex and CFD accounts while Trade The Pool evaluates equities accounts. That difference is prior to every other comparison: the sessions, position sizing, costs and gap risk are not the same product category. A trader who ignores this and compares only the headline target is comparing two different sports by their scoreboards.

Forex hours against the US equity session

FTMO evaluates forex and CFD accounts on weekday forex hours; Trade The Pool evaluates equity day-trading accounts on the US cash session. The instruments, the platform stack and the kind of news that moves price are different. A forex trader who has never handled an equity halt or a single-stock gap should not treat the equity product as a cheaper version of the same job.

The loss mechanics are different too. FTMO uses a static percentage floor measured from the starting balance and a separate equity daily stop. Trade The Pool typically uses a hard dollar loss limit with a daily stop and a profit target. The static floor is easier to size against because it does not move with profit; the fixed dollar limit is also stable, but the daily stop and equity-specific gaps change how a session behaves.

Sizing on a $50,000 account

On a $50,000 FTMO account with a 10% maximum loss, the floor is $45,000 and a 5% daily stop is $2,500. A $250 risk unit has ten losses of room to the daily stop and twenty to the total floor. On a $50,000 equity account with a $2,500 fixed loss limit, the same $250 unit also has ten losses of room, but a single halt or gap can consume more than one unit at once. That is why the daily stop matters more on the equity side.

The profit target adds another difference. A percentage target on a forex account is a fixed dollar distance from the starting balance; an equity target may be measured differently and may interact with the daily stop. Read the target definition, not just the percentage, before calculating how many winning trades the plan needs.

Billing also differs: FTMO charges once per attempt, while Trade The Pool charges a monthly subscription. A slow pass on the equity product carries a calendar cost that the forex product does not.

Which market belongs in your week

FTMO fits a forex trader who wants a static floor, a conventional two-phase path and a wide platform choice. Trade The Pool fits an equity day trader who already understands the cash session and wants a fixed loss limit. A trader with no equity experience should start elsewhere; the evaluation fee is the smallest cost of learning a new market under a deadline.

If you trade both markets already, compare the rulebooks on the row that would end your last attempt. For most traders that is the daily stop, not the headline target.

Common questions

Can I trade forex on Trade The Pool?

No. Trade The Pool evaluates equity day-trading accounts. Forex pairs are not part of that product, and the session and platform stack are built around US equities.

Does FTMO have a daily loss limit?

Yes. FTMO applies an equity-based daily loss limit in addition to the static maximum loss. The daily limit can end a session before the total floor is reached.

Which product charges monthly?

Trade The Pool charges a monthly subscription on the product compared here. FTMO charges once per attempt, so a slow pass does not add monthly fees.
