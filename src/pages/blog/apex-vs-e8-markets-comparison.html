Apex Trader Funding vs E8 Markets: which rulebook fits your trading

Apex Trader Funding and E8 Markets are compared on the rows that decide an attempt: the loss floor, the daily stop, the billing rhythm and the payout conditions. They evaluate different markets — futures versus forex and CFD — which changes the sessions, the sizing and the gap risk before any headline target is relevant. Numbers below describe each published rulebook's shape; confirm the current plan schedule before buying.

Key takeaways

  • E8 Markets: static on most plans, one-off per attempt.
  • Daily stops: no separate daily stop on the standard evaluation versus yes, percentage based.

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. Apex Trader Funding uses a trailing, measured from peak equity including open profit floor, no separate daily stop on the standard evaluation daily stop and monthly subscription per account; E8 Markets uses a static on most plans floor, yes, percentage based daily stop and one-off per attempt. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

Apex Trader Funding evaluates futures accounts while E8 Markets evaluates forex and CFD accounts. That difference is prior to every other comparison: the sessions, position sizing, costs and gap risk are not the same product category. A trader who ignores this and compares only the headline target is comparing two different sports by their scoreboards.

Futures trail, forex usually stays put

Apex evaluates futures with a threshold that follows peak equity including open profit. E8 Markets evaluates forex and CFD accounts, with a static floor on most plans and both one-step and two-phase structures. The market difference is prior to the price difference: a futures trader on Apex works the US session with contract specifications, while an E8 trader works weekday forex hours with lot-based sizing.

The loss mechanics reinforce the split. Apex's trailing floor changes after every new equity high, so the room available for a retracement shrinks as the account earns. E8's static floor stays at the initial distance on most plans, which makes the dollar risk easier to hold constant. A trader who has only used static forex evaluations often underestimates how much the Apex trail changes the last third of an attempt.

The same $50,000, two different size tables

On a $50,000 Apex account with a $2,500 trailing threshold, a $250 risk unit has ten losses of room at the start. After a $1,000 gain, the floor rises and the room from the original balance falls to $1,500, or six losses at the same size. On a $50,000 E8 account with a 10% static floor, the failure point is $45,000 and a $250 unit keeps twenty losses of room for the whole attempt, even after a gain.

The correct Apex size after the gain is lower, not because the strategy changed but because the floor did. A trader who keeps the original size is gradually increasing risk relative to the remaining cushion. On the static account, the size can stay constant, which is one reason static forex evaluations are easier for a systematic trader to model.

Billing differs as well: Apex charges monthly per account, E8 charges once per attempt on most plans. A slow pass on Apex adds subscription cost; a slow pass on E8 does not.

Which product fits

Apex fits a futures trader who can watch the US session and wants a large account ladder with a monthly subscription. E8 fits a forex trader who wants a static floor, a one-off fee and a choice between one-step and two-phase structures. A trader who cannot decide between markets should start with the one they have actually traded, because the rulebook is easier to learn than an unfamiliar session.

If you use both, keep separate journals and separate size tables. The fact that both accounts are called funded does not make the risk math the same.
