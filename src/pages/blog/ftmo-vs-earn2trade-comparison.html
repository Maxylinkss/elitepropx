FTMO vs Earn2Trade: the two rulebooks side by side

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. FTMO uses a static, measured from the starting balance floor, yes, equity-based daily stop and one-off per attempt; Earn2Trade uses a end-of-day trailing on most programmes floor, yes daily stop and one-off per programme. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

FTMO evaluates forex and CFD accounts while Earn2Trade evaluates futures accounts. That difference is prior to every other comparison: the sessions, position sizing, costs and gap risk are not the same product category. A trader who ignores this and compares only the headline target is comparing two different sports by their scoreboards.

A forex percentage rulebook against a futures programme

FTMO evaluates forex and CFD accounts with a static percentage loss floor and an equity daily stop. Earn2Trade evaluates futures accounts with a programme that includes structured education and an end-of-day trailing threshold on most offers. The market difference decides the session and the platform; the trailing mechanic decides how position size changes after a winning day.

The education bundle is the second difference. Part of the Earn2Trade fee buys a course, whether or not the trader needs it. FTMO sells the evaluation and the platform access, with no curriculum attached. For a beginner, the course can be the reason to choose Earn2Trade; for an experienced trader, it is a cost with no corresponding benefit.

A $100,000 attempt and a held winner

On a $100,000 FTMO account with a 10% floor, the failure point is $90,000 and stays there. A $500 risk unit has twenty losses of room. On a $100,000 Earn2Trade account with a $3,000 end-of-day trailing threshold, the same $500 unit has six losses of room at the start. A $1,000 winner held through the session does not move the Earn2Trade floor until the close; at a firm with an intraday trail, it would.

That distinction is the main reason a futures trader might prefer the end-of-day version. The floor still moves after a profitable close, so the size must be recalculated the next morning. A trader who sets size once and forgets it will gradually take more risk relative to the remaining cushion.

Billing differs as well: FTMO charges once per attempt; Earn2Trade charges once per programme. A slow pass on the futures side does not add a monthly subscription, but the programme price includes the course.

The trader each product fits

FTMO fits a forex trader who wants a conventional static rulebook, a wide platform list and no course attached. Earn2Trade fits a beginner who wants a futures evaluation with instruction and an end-of-day trailing rule. An experienced futures trader who already has a method may find the course redundant and prefer a product that sells only the evaluation.

If you are choosing between the two as a first evaluation, start with the market you can trade in your available hours. The rulebook is easier to learn than a new session and a new instrument set.

Common questions

Is the Earn2Trade trailing threshold intraday or end-of-day?

Most Earn2Trade programmes use an end-of-day trailing threshold, so open profit does not move the floor until the session closes. Confirm the exact programme before relying on that behaviour.

Does FTMO include education?

The evaluation does not bundle a structured course. FTMO publishes extensive documentation, but it is not the same product as a paid curriculum.

Which one is cheaper for a slow pass?

FTMO charges once per attempt and Earn2Trade charges once per programme on most offers, so neither adds a monthly fee. The total depends on the number of attempts and what the programme bundle includes.
