How the Funded Engineer rulebook is built

The Funded Engineer evaluation is best understood as a set of constraints that interact, not as a headline profit target with some footnotes. The target decides how much you have to make. The loss floor decides how much room you have to make it. The daily stop, if there is one, decides how quickly a bad session ends. The minimum-day rule decides how long the attempt takes even when the target is reached early. Change any one of those and the same trading strategy produces a different outcome.

For Funded Engineer, the published structure is a two-phase evaluation with a static floor. The loss floor is static, the daily stop is yes, and the consistency condition is yes. Billing is one-off per attempt, payouts follow profit split on a fixed schedule, and the supported platforms are MT4, MT5. Those are the rows that matter. Marketing pages tend to foreground the profit target and the profit split because both are easy to compare; neither tells you whether a given trade is survivable.

A static forex floor with a consistency condition

Funded Engineer runs a two-phase forex evaluation with a static floor and a consistency test. The static floor is the easier part: the dollar distance to failure does not move when the account earns, so a fixed risk unit stays valid for the whole attempt. The consistency test is the part that catches traders at the payout stage, because a pass built on one or two oversized days can satisfy the target and still fail the distribution check.

The practical response is to plan the profit distribution before the first trade. If the plan requires that no single day contributes more than a set share of the total, the target has to be reached through a series of normal days rather than one exceptional one. That changes the trade selection: a setup with a large expected payoff but a low hit rate is less useful than a smaller, repeatable edge.

A $100,000 attempt with a 10% target

On a $100,000 account with a 10% target, the plan needs $10,000 of profit. If the consistency rule caps a single day at 25% of the total, no day can contribute more than $2,500 without risking the payout. At a $500 risk unit and a 2:1 plan, a normal winning day of two winners contributes $2,000, which fits. A single $4,000 day would breach the distribution even though it moves the balance closer to the target.

The static floor gives the trader room to build that distribution: a 10% maximum loss means twenty losses at $500, so the plan can absorb a normal losing streak without the failure point moving. The binding constraint is the calendar and the consistency rule, not the floor.

Billing is one-off per attempt, so a slower, more evenly distributed pass does not add fees. That is an advantage for a trader who needs time to build a consistent record.

What to verify before buying

Confirm the consistency formula: is it a share of total profit, a share of the target, or a maximum day relative to the average? Confirm whether it applies to the evaluation, the funded account or only the payout. Confirm the daily stop and its reset time. Confirm the platform list and whether MT4 and MT5 are both available on the plan. Finally, confirm the payout cycle and the minimum withdrawal, because a pass that cannot be withdrawn is not a pass.

Ask for the clause in writing. A support reply that quotes the terms is the only version of the rule you can rely on later.

Common questions

Does Funded Engineer use a static or trailing floor?

The evaluation described here uses a static floor. Profit does not move the failure point, which makes a fixed risk unit easier to maintain for the whole attempt.

What is the consistency test?

It limits how much of the profit can come from a single day or a small number of days. A pass built on one oversized winner can fail the test even when the target is met.
