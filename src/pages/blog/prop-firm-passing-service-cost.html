The return on the fee, honestly

What the flat fee covers

A flat passing fee covers the work of trading an evaluation to its target under the firm's rules. It usually includes the risk plan, the execution, the progress reporting and the retry policy stated in the quote. It does not include the evaluation fee unless the quote says so, and it does not include platform or data costs charged by the firm. The first step in comparing two quotes is to line up the inclusions.

The fee is flat because the work is the same regardless of account size. A $100,000 evaluation and a $200,000 evaluation require the same rule reading, the same risk plan and the same execution discipline; the dollar risk changes, but the process does not. That is why a per-account-size fee is harder to justify than a flat fee, and why the flat fee is easier to audit.

The per-size economics

On a $50,000 account, a $220 fee is 0.44% of the account. On a $200,000 account, it is 0.11%. The fee does not scale with the account, so the relative cost falls as the size rises. That makes the service more attractive on larger accounts, provided the strategy can trade the larger size without changing its risk profile. A method that works at $50,000 may need wider stops or smaller relative size at $200,000.

The evaluation fee does scale with size, and so does the platform cost. Add those to the service fee before comparing a small account with a large one. A large account with a high evaluation fee can still be the cheaper route per dollar of funded capital, but the absolute cash outlay is larger.

When the service is the wrong buy

If the firm prohibits third-party trading, the service is the wrong buy regardless of the price. If the client wants to learn the rulebook, the service removes the learning opportunity. If the client has a tested method and a high pass probability, self-trading is usually cheaper. The service is a fit for a trader who values time, has a firm that permits the arrangement and does not want to manage the evaluation personally.

Ask for the terms check and the retry policy before paying. A quote that cannot name the clause it relies on is not a quote worth accepting.

Common questions

Why is the fee flat instead of a percentage?

The work is the same process at any account size: read the rulebook, build the risk plan and execute. A flat fee reflects that process rather than the size of the account.

Does the fee include the evaluation?

Only if the quote says so. Many quotes cover the service work and leave the evaluation fee to the client. Compare the inclusions before comparing totals.

Is the service worth it on a small account?

The fee is a larger share of a small account, so the relative cost is higher. It can still be worth it if the time saved matters, but the math favors larger accounts.
