FTMO vs Audacity Capital: which rulebook fits your trading

FTMO and Audacity Capital are compared on the rows that decide an attempt: the loss floor, the daily stop, the billing rhythm and the payout conditions. They evaluate the same broad market, so the decision turns on mechanics and commercial terms. Numbers below describe each published rulebook's shape; confirm the current plan schedule before buying.

Key takeaways

  • Audacity Capital: firm-specific, often a fixed loss allowance, one-off or profit-share based depending on programme.
  • Daily stops: yes, equity-based versus programme-specific.

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. FTMO uses a static, measured from the starting balance floor, yes, equity-based daily stop and one-off per attempt; Audacity Capital uses a firm-specific, often a fixed loss allowance floor, programme-specific daily stop and one-off or profit-share based depending on programme. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

A checkout challenge against a reviewed allocation

FTMO is a self-serve evaluation: you buy a plan, trade two phases and a verification phase, and the rulebook is the same for every buyer on that plan. Audacity Capital is closer to a capital allocation programme, with an application and a review before trading starts. That difference changes the calendar before it changes the risk: a trader comparing the two is not comparing two checkouts but a checkout and an interview.

The loss allowance is expressed differently as well. FTMO publishes a percentage floor measured from the starting balance, which makes the dollar figure easy to calculate at any account size. Audacity's programme usually states a fixed dollar loss allowance, which is simpler to read but harder to compare across account sizes. Convert both to dollars before deciding which one gives more room.

What the fixed allowance does to position size

Suppose FTMO's $100,000 plan carries a 10% maximum loss and a 5% daily stop. The total floor is $90,000 and the daily limit is $5,000. At a 0.5% risk unit of $500, the day can absorb ten losses before the daily stop, but the total floor can absorb twenty. The daily rule binds first on a bad day, and the total rule binds first on a bad month.

A fixed dollar allowance of, say, $10,000 on a $100,000 allocation produces the same starting arithmetic, but the absence of a percentage formula means the number does not change when the balance changes. That is easier to plan around, and it is also less forgiving of a trader who sizes as a percentage of current equity: after a drawdown the percentage method risks less in dollars, which slows recovery, while the fixed allowance still permits the original size.

Audacity's programme also carries a minimum trading period, so a fast, high-conviction pass may not be accepted on the original timeline. FTMO's verification phase has a similar effect on the calendar but not on the loss allowance. Neither is a reason to avoid the firm; both are reasons to map the calendar before paying.

Which route fits which trader

FTMO fits a trader who wants a conventional, documented rulebook, a self-serve dashboard and the widest platform choice. Audacity fits an experienced forex trader who prefers a reviewed allocation and a fixed dollar loss allowance to a percentage challenge, and who is willing to wait for approval before the first trade. A beginner should generally start with the self-serve product because the review process adds a variable that has nothing to do with trading skill.

If the goal is to reach a funded account as quickly as possible, FTMO's documented process is easier to schedule. If the goal is a longer relationship with a fixed risk budget, Audacity's structure is closer to what a proprietary desk actually offers. The mistake is treating the two as interchangeable because both appear in the same comparison tables.

Common questions

Is Audacity Capital a standard prop firm challenge?

Not in the checkout sense. It runs a reviewed capital allocation programme with an application stage, a fixed loss allowance and programme-specific terms, so it should be compared with other allocation programmes rather than with a self-serve evaluation.

Does FTMO use a trailing or static loss floor?

FTMO uses a static maximum loss measured from the starting balance. Profit does not move the failure point, which makes the dollar risk easier to calculate for the whole attempt.

Which one is better for a beginner?

FTMO is usually the easier first product because the rulebook is public, the process is self-serve and the platform choice is wider. Audacity suits traders who already have a record and prefer a reviewed allocation.
