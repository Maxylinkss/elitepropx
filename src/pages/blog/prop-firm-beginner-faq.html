  1. What this guide is really solving

What this guide is really solving

A first evaluation should be chosen for the clarity of its rulebook rather than the size of its headline account. A small account with a static floor teaches the mechanics at a lower cost than a large account with a trailing threshold. On an evaluation, the consequence is specific: the same decision either keeps the loss floor at a distance or brings the end of the account closer. The sections below turn that into a plan rather than a principle.

The questions beginners ask first

The first question is not which firm has the biggest account; it is which rulebook you can explain in one sentence. A static percentage floor with a daily stop is easier to learn than a trailing threshold with a consistency condition. Start with the simpler rulebook and a small account. The cost of a first evaluation should be small enough that a failure is tuition, not a crisis.

The second question is how much to risk per trade. A beginner should use 0.25% to 0.5% of the account, not 1% to 2%. The smaller size leaves room for the learning curve and for a normal losing streak. A trader who risks 2% on a first evaluation can breach the daily stop in three losses, which is a normal day.

What a first evaluation should teach

A first evaluation is a process test. The goal is to learn how the rulebook behaves, how the platform executes and how the trader responds to a daily stop. The pass is a bonus. A beginner who treats the first evaluation as a pass-or-fail exam will take risks that the process cannot support. A beginner who treats it as a supervised rehearsal will learn something that transfers to the next attempt.

Keep a journal from the first trade: setup, risk, stop, result and remaining room. The journal is the asset that survives a failed attempt. After the attempt, review the journal before buying another evaluation. If the losses came from the process, fix the process; if they came from the market, the process may be fine.

When to buy a second attempt

Buy a second attempt only after answering two questions: what caused the first failure, and what changed in the process? If the answer is "I got unlucky", the process has not been reviewed. If the answer is "I risked too much after a loss and hit the daily stop", the change is a smaller risk unit and a personal daily stop. The second attempt should test the change, not repeat the first attempt with more hope.

If the first attempt was failed by a rule misunderstanding, the second attempt should start with a written rule summary and a support confirmation. The fee is the same; the lesson should not be.

Common questions

Which prop firm is best for a beginner?

The one with the simplest rulebook and a low-cost small account. A static floor with a daily stop is easier to learn than a trailing threshold with a consistency condition.

How much should a beginner risk per trade?

0.25% to 0.5% of the account. A larger size can breach the daily stop in a normal losing run before the method has a chance to work.

Should I buy another evaluation after failing?

Only after identifying the cause and changing the process. Repeating the same attempt with the same size is not a new test.
