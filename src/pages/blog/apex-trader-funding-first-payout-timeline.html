From qualifying day to money in the bank

The first Apex payout is a sequence, not a button. The account has to reach the qualifying profit and the required number of trading days, then the payout request enters a review, then the payment is processed. Each stage has its own delay, and the total is what the trader should budget for. A plan that assumes cash arrives the day after the request is a plan that will be wrong by at least a week.

Start by counting the qualifying days on the plan you actually bought. The count is not the same as the number of days you logged in; a day usually has to meet a minimum activity or profit condition. Mark the qualifying days on a calendar as they happen, because reconstructing them from memory at payout time is how a request gets rejected for a clerical reason.

Building a payout buffer before the request

The request is not the end of the risk. The account continues to trade, and a breach after the request but before the payment can still affect the payout. Leave a buffer above the floor and above the target: if the plan requires a minimum balance after withdrawal, withdrawing the maximum can push the account below the next threshold or reset the qualifying period. Read the post-payout terms before deciding the amount.

A first payout should be treated as a proof of process, not as a payday. Take an amount that leaves the account comfortably above the floor, confirm the processor fee, and keep the statement. The second payout is where the rhythm becomes predictable; the first one is where the paperwork is learned.

The three delays to plan for

There is a qualifying delay (the days before a request is allowed), a review delay (the time support takes to approve the request) and a processing delay (the time the payment provider takes to settle). The qualifying delay is in the plan terms, the review delay is operational, and the processing delay depends on the method. Ask support for the typical review and processing windows in writing, then add a margin.

If the payout is time-sensitive, choose the method with the shortest settlement and accept the fee. A cheaper method that takes a week longer is not cheaper if the money is needed sooner. The right choice depends on the purpose of the withdrawal, not on the fee table alone.

Common questions

How long does the first Apex payout take?

It depends on three stages: qualifying days, request review and payment processing. The plan terms define the qualifying stage, while the review and processing windows are operational. Ask support for the current windows and add a margin.

Can I withdraw the full profit on the first payout?

The plan may require a minimum balance to remain after withdrawal. Read the post-payout terms before requesting the maximum, because a withdrawal that leaves the account too close to the floor can affect the next cycle.

Do I need to keep trading after the request?

The account remains subject to the rules until the payout is complete. Avoid taking risk that could breach the floor between the request and the payment.
