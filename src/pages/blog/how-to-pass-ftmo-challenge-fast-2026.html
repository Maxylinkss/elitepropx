  1. What this guide is really solving

What this guide is really solving

A one-day pass is possible only where the plan has no minimum-day rule and the target can be reached inside a single session without breaching the daily stop. It is a structural possibility, not a strategy, and a pass built on one oversized trade often fails a consistency test later. On an evaluation, the consequence is specific: the same decision either keeps the loss floor at a distance or brings the end of the account closer. The sections below turn that into a plan rather than a principle.

Fast is a pacing decision, not a size decision

The fastest legitimate way to pass an FTMO challenge is to use a larger number of high-quality setups at a controlled risk, not to increase the risk per trade. FTMO's static floor and daily stop do not reward a larger size; they punish a breach. The trader who doubles the risk to finish in a week is twice as likely to hit the daily stop and no more likely to reach the target.

The pacing decision is about the number of trading days and the number of valid setups. If the method produces two valid setups a day and the target requires twenty net winners, the minimum is ten days of perfect execution. A realistic plan allows for losses and missed days, so the calendar is longer. The speed comes from taking the valid setups, not from forcing more.

The target math on a $100,000 account

On a $100,000 account with a 10% target, the account needs $10,000. At a $500 risk unit and a 2:1 plan, a winning trade contributes $1,000, so ten net winners are needed. At a 1:2 plan, a winner contributes $500 and the count doubles. The faster route is the higher reward-to-risk plan, provided the win rate does not collapse. A 2:1 plan with a 40% win rate has a positive expectancy; a 4:1 plan with a 20% win rate may have the same, but the losing streaks are longer.

The daily stop is the constraint on a fast attempt. If the trader takes too many setups in one day and hits the daily limit, the account is done for the day. The plan should cap the number of trades at a level that keeps the daily budget intact even after a normal losing run.

What not to do

Do not increase size after a losing day to recover. Do not hold a position through a news event unless the plan permits it. Do not skip the verification phase in the calendar. Do not assume that a fast pass is a better pass; the payout stage is the same. A trader who reaches the target in a week but fails the verification or the payout review has not passed.

If the goal is speed, the best investment is preparation: a tested setup, a written risk plan and a journal from the first trade. Preparation shortens the calendar more reliably than a larger position.

Common questions

Can I pass FTMO in one week?

It is possible if the target is reached with valid setups and the risk stays within the daily stop, but it is not a reliable plan. The verification phase and payout seasoning still apply, so a fast evaluation does not mean fast cash.

Should I increase risk to pass faster?

No. A larger risk increases the chance of hitting the daily stop and does not improve the expectancy. Speed comes from valid setups and preparation, not from size.

What is the fastest legitimate route?

A tested high-reward-to-risk setup traded at a controlled size, with enough valid setups per week to reach the target without breaching the daily stop.
