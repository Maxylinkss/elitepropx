What this guide is really solving

The rules that matter are the ones that change a decision: the loss floor changes size, the daily stop changes when to stop, the minimum days change the calendar and the consistency rule changes the distribution. Everything else is commentary. On an evaluation, the consequence is specific: the same decision either keeps the loss floor at a distance or brings the end of the account closer. The sections below turn that into a plan rather than a principle.

The mistake that ends most attempts

The most common mistake is sizing against the account balance instead of the remaining room. On a trailing floor, a trader who keeps a 1% risk unit after a gain is taking a larger share of the cushion than at the start. The account can be closed by a normal retracement even though the balance is still positive. The fix is to recalculate the risk unit from the distance to the floor before every session.

The second most common mistake is treating the daily stop as a target. A trader who tries to recover a loss in the same session usually increases size and hits the limit. The daily stop is a circuit breaker; the session should end before it is reached. A personal three-loss rule is easier to follow than a percentage limit.

Process mistakes that look like bad luck

Taking a trade without a stop, moving a stop away from the entry, and holding a position through a restricted news window are process mistakes, not bad luck. Each one can end an account regardless of the profit. The evaluation is not a test of whether the trader can predict the market; it is a test of whether the trader can follow a rulebook under pressure. A process mistake is a failure of the test even when the trade wins.

The fix is a pre-trade checklist: remaining room, daily loss used, stop distance, size and news window. If any item is unknown, the trade is not ready. The checklist takes a minute and catches the mistakes that cost the fee.

The review that prevents the repeat

After a failed attempt, review the journal before buying another evaluation. Categorize each loss as a setup loss, a sizing error, a rule breach or an execution error. The categories tell you what to change. A setup loss is part of the method; a sizing error is a process bug; a rule breach is a reading failure; an execution error is a platform or discipline problem. Each has a different fix.

A trader who cannot categorize the losses has not reviewed the attempt. The next evaluation will repeat the same pattern with a different date. The fee is the price of the lesson; the review is how the lesson is collected.

Common questions

What is the biggest mistake in a prop firm challenge?

Sizing against the account balance instead of the remaining room, especially on a trailing floor. A normal retracement can then end the account while the balance is still positive.

Why is moving a stop a problem?

It changes the risk of the trade after entry and can breach the plan’s conduct rules. It also turns a planned loss into an unplanned one.

How do I avoid repeating the same failure?

Categorize each loss in the journal as a setup loss, sizing error, rule breach or execution error. Each category has a different fix, and the review is what makes the next attempt different.
