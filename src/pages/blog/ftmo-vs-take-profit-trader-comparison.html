Two markets, two completely different loss mechanics

FTMO evaluates forex and CFD accounts on a percentage rulebook with a static maximum loss. Take Profit Trader evaluates futures accounts on a trailing threshold with a payout-focused rule set. The market difference comes before every other row: forex sessions are weekdays and can be traded from a laptop, while futures sessions follow the US clock and often require a data feed and a platform built for futures.

The loss mechanic is the second filter. FTMO's static floor stays at the starting level, so a winning trade does not make the failure point closer. Take Profit Trader's trailing floor follows peak equity, which means a profitable run reduces the room available for a normal retracement. A trader who has only traded static forex evaluations will find the futures rule more demanding even if the headline target looks similar.

The same trade on two different accounts

Take a $400 risk unit on a $100,000 account. At FTMO, the maximum loss is $10,000, so the account can absorb twenty-five losing trades before the floor is reached. At Take Profit Trader, a $3,000 trailing threshold gives seven losing trades of room at the same size, and the number falls after every new equity high. A trader who uses the FTMO size on the futures account will breach long before the target.

The correct size on the trailing account is derived from the remaining room, not the original threshold. If the floor is $2,100 below the current equity after a good run, a 0.5% risk of $500 has only four losses of room. Cutting to $250 restores eight. That adjustment is the whole skill of trading a trailing product, and it is the reason the two accounts cannot share one position-size table.

Billing adds a second difference: FTMO charges once per attempt, while Take Profit Trader charges a monthly subscription. A slow pass on the futures side pays for calendar time; a slow pass on the forex side does not. Add the expected number of months to the cost comparison before deciding which is cheaper.

The trader each rulebook actually fits

FTMO fits a forex trader who wants a static floor, a conventional two-phase path and a platform list that includes MT4, MT5, cTrader and DXtrade. Take Profit Trader fits a futures trader who can trade the US session, understands that the floor follows equity, and wants a payout-focused rulebook with a monthly billing rhythm. A trader who cannot watch the US session should not buy the futures product regardless of its advertised pass rate.

Do not choose based on the profit split. The split applies to a payout that only exists if the account survives, and the account survives only if the loss mechanic matches the strategy. For most traders, the market and the session are the deciding rows, not the percentage printed beside the payout.

Common questions

Can a forex strategy be used on Take Profit Trader?

The product evaluates futures accounts, so the instruments, session hours and contract specifications are different from spot forex. A strategy built around forex sessions and pip values needs to be re-tested on futures before it is used on the evaluation.

Does FTMO move its loss floor after a winning trade?

No. FTMO uses a static maximum loss measured from the starting balance. A winning trade changes the balance but not the failure point, so the distance to the floor increases.

Why does the trailing floor matter more on a futures account?

Futures contracts have a fixed dollar value per point, so position size translates directly into dollars of risk. A trailing floor that follows open profit can be reached by a normal retracement even when the closed balance is still positive.
