Apex Trader Funding vs Earn2Trade: which rulebook fits your trading

Apex Trader Funding and Earn2Trade are compared on the rows that decide an attempt: the loss floor, the daily stop, the billing rhythm and the payout conditions. They evaluate the same broad market, so the decision turns on mechanics and commercial terms. Numbers below describe each published rulebook's shape; confirm the current plan schedule before buying.

Key takeaways

  • Earn2Trade: end-of-day trailing on most programmes, one-off per programme.

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. Apex Trader Funding uses a trailing, measured from peak equity including open profit floor, no separate daily stop on the standard evaluation daily stop and monthly subscription per account; Earn2Trade uses a end-of-day trailing on most programmes floor, yes daily stop and one-off per programme. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

Choose Earn2Trade if your strategy fits a end-of-day trailing loss floor, you can trade its session (US futures session hours) and its billing rhythm (one-off per programme) matches your timeline. It is specifically suited to beginners who want a futures evaluation with a course attached. The strongest reason to pick it is end-of-day trailing is easier to trade than an intraday trail.

An intraday trail against an end-of-day trail

Both firms evaluate futures accounts, but the trailing mechanic is not the same. Apex measures its threshold from peak equity including open profit, so an unrealised winner can raise the floor during the session. Earn2Trade uses an end-of-day trailing threshold on most programmes, which means open profit does not move the floor until the session closes. For a trader who holds a winner through the day, the end-of-day version is materially more forgiving.

The second difference is what comes bundled. Earn2Trade attaches a structured futures course to its programme, which is useful for a beginner and unnecessary for a trader who already has a tested method. Apex sells the evaluation and the platform access; education is not part of the product. That changes the value calculation: part of the Earn2Trade fee buys instruction, whether or not the buyer needs it.

A $100,000 attempt with a $1,000 daily winner

Take a $100,000 account with a $3,000 trailing threshold and a $1,000 winning trade held open. At Apex, the floor rises toward $98,000 while the trade is open, leaving a smaller cushion for a retracement. At Earn2Trade, the floor stays at $97,000 until the session closes; if the trade is closed at $1,000, the floor then adjusts to the new end-of-day balance. The difference is not cosmetic: a $900 retracement that would end the Apex account is survivable on the end-of-day rule.

Position sizing has to follow the mechanic. On the Apex account, a $300 risk unit has ten losses of room at the start but fewer after a run. On the Earn2Trade account, the same $300 unit has ten losses of room against a floor that only moves once a day. A trader who uses one size on both accounts is not running the same strategy, even if the entry signals are identical.

The billing difference is smaller than the mechanic difference. Apex charges monthly per account; Earn2Trade charges once per programme. A fast pass favours the one-off, a slow pass favours neither automatically because the monthly cost depends on the number of months and the one-off includes the course.

Who should pick which

Apex fits a self-directed futures trader who wants a large account ladder, a monthly subscription and a rulebook that rewards discipline over activity. Earn2Trade fits a beginner who wants a course alongside the evaluation and prefers an end-of-day trailing rule that does not punish an open winner. An experienced trader who already has a method may resent paying for the course; a beginner who buys Apex may underestimate how much the trailing rule demands.

Whichever is chosen, confirm the exact programme name before comparing prices. Both firms sell more than one generation of terms, and the trailing mechanic can differ between a standard programme and a promotional one.

Common questions

Is Earn2Trade easier than Apex because it uses end-of-day trailing?

It is more forgiving for a trader who holds winners through the session, because open profit does not move the floor until the close. It is not automatically easier overall: the programme rules, minimum days and payout conditions still apply.

Does the Earn2Trade fee include a course?

Yes, the programme bundles structured futures education. That is part of what the fee buys, so an experienced trader should compare the evaluation-only value rather than the full price.

Which firm charges monthly?

Apex charges a monthly subscription per account. Earn2Trade charges once per programme on most offers. A slow pass therefore has a different cost profile on each.
