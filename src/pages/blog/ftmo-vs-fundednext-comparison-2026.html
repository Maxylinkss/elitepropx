One conventional path against a plan menu

FTMO sells one documented path: two evaluation phases, a verification phase, a static percentage floor and an equity daily stop. FundedNext sells a menu of one-step and two-phase models, with static floors on the two-phase model and trailing floors on some one-step variants. The comparison is therefore not one rulebook against another but one rulebook against a family of them.

The practical consequence is that the FundedNext plan name is part of the rule. A trader who buys the cheapest one-step offer may be taking a trailing floor with a tighter allowance, while a trader on the two-phase model gets a static floor and a longer path. The fee can be the same across the brand; the risk is not.

Sizing a $100,000 attempt

On FTMO's $100,000 plan with a 10% maximum loss, the floor is $90,000 and a 5% daily stop is $5,000. A $500 risk unit has ten losses of room to the daily stop and twenty to the total floor. On a FundedNext two-phase plan with a static floor of the same size, the arithmetic is the same. On a one-step variant with a 6% floor, the failure point is $94,000 and the same $500 unit has twelve losses of room; if the variant trails, a gain reduces that room further.

FTMO's verification phase adds calendar time but not risk. FundedNext's one-step model removes a phase but compresses the allowance. A trader who needs speed may prefer the one-step model; a trader who needs margin for error should take the two-phase plan or FTMO.

Both firms bill once per attempt, so the entry fee does not grow with the calendar. The cost of a slow pass is time, not another monthly payment.

Which trader should pick which

FTMO fits a trader who wants one well-documented rulebook, the widest platform choice and a conventional verification path. FundedNext fits a trader who understands the difference between its models and wants to choose between speed and a softer floor. A trader who cannot state the floor definition of the plan they bought should not be comparing the two brands yet.

If the two-phase FundedNext plan and FTMO are close on price, choose on platform and payout operations. Both are static, both are one-off, and both require the trader to keep the risk unit stable. The brand is less important than the plan name and the payout rhythm.

Common questions

Is the FundedNext two-phase plan static?

The two-phase model is commonly described as static. Some one-step variants use a trailing floor, so the plan name has to be checked before comparing it with FTMO.

Does FTMO have a one-step plan?

The standard FTMO path is two evaluation phases followed by a verification phase. It is not a one-step product, so a direct speed comparison with a FundedNext one-step plan is not like for like.

Which firm has a verification phase?

FTMO includes a verification phase after the two evaluation phases. The FundedNext two-phase model does not use the same structure, and its one-step model removes a phase entirely.
