  1. What this guide is really solving

What this guide is really solving

Risk management on an evaluation is the practice of keeping the probability of ruin below the probability of reaching the target. That is a different objective from maximising expected return, and it usually requires smaller size than a personal account would use. On an evaluation, the consequence is specific: the same decision either keeps the loss floor at a distance or brings the end of the account closer. The sections below turn that into a plan rather than a principle.

Risk management is a budget, not a feeling

The loss limit is a budget, and every trade spends part of it. If the account has $10,000 of total room and the plan is to risk $500 per trade, the budget allows twenty losses. The daily stop may allow ten. The binding number is the smaller of the two on any given day, and the plan should be built around it rather than around the total.

A budget makes the sizing decision mechanical. Divide the dollar risk by the stop distance and the instrument value, and the lot size is fixed. If the result is too large for the platform's minimum or too small to be meaningful, the setup is not suitable for the account. The trader does not need a stronger opinion; they need a different setup or a different account size.

The losing-streak calculation

Before the first trade, estimate the longest losing streak the method has produced in a representative sample. If the sample's worst streak is eight losses, a budget that allows only ten is too tight; a normal streak will almost reach the floor. A budget that allows twenty gives the method room to be wrong twice as long as the sample.

The calculation is not a prediction. It is a margin of safety. A method with a 45% win rate will produce long losing streaks by chance, even if the edge is real. The trader who sizes for the average streak is the trader who breaches during a normal bad month.

The daily stop as a circuit breaker

The firm's daily stop is a limit, not a target. A trader should set a personal stop below it, usually three losses or a fixed percentage of the daily budget. The personal stop is a circuit breaker: it ends the session before the emotional decisions begin. The firm's stop is the consequence; the personal stop is the control.

Write the daily loss used and the remaining budget before each entry. A trader who can state both numbers from memory is far less likely to take a revenge trade. The journal is not a record of opinions; it is the risk dashboard.

Common questions

What percentage should I risk per trade?

Most traders should risk 0.25% to 0.5% of the account per trade on a static floor. On a trailing floor, the size should be recalculated from the remaining room after a gain.

How many losses should the account be able to absorb?

At least twice the worst losing streak in a representative sample. If the method has produced eight consecutive losses, a budget of ten is too tight; twenty gives a margin of safety.

Should I use the firm’s daily stop as my own?

No. Set a personal stop below the firm’s limit, such as three losses or a fixed share of the daily budget. The firm’s stop is the consequence, not the plan.
