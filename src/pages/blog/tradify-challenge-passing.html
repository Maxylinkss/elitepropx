How the Tradify rulebook is built

The Tradify evaluation is best understood as a set of constraints that interact, not as a headline profit target with some footnotes. The target decides how much you have to make. The loss floor decides how much room you have to make it. The daily stop, if there is one, decides how quickly a bad session ends. The minimum-day rule decides how long the attempt takes even when the target is reached early. Change any one of those and the same trading strategy produces a different outcome.

For Tradify, the published structure is a evaluation with a trailing threshold and a daily stop. The loss floor is trailing, with a session stop, the daily stop is yes, and the consistency condition is yes. Billing is monthly subscription, payouts follow payouts after qualifying days, and the supported platforms are Tradovate, TradingView, NinjaTrader. Those are the rows that matter. Marketing pages tend to foreground the profit target and the profit split because both are easy to compare; neither tells you whether a given trade is survivable.

A daily stop changes the failure mode

Tradify combines a trailing threshold with a session stop. The daily stop is the rule that ends a bad day; the trailing floor is the rule that ends a bad sequence. A trader who understands only one of them will mis-size the account. On a session where the daily stop is $1,500 and the normal risk unit is $300, the day can absorb five losses. That is the effective limit for the session, regardless of how much total room remains.

The trailing floor then decides the next session. If the account made $900 yesterday, the floor has moved up and the room from the original balance is smaller. The size that was appropriate at the start may now be too large relative to the cushion. Recalculate the remaining room each morning and divide by the risk unit before the first trade.

The order of operations for a Tradify attempt

Start with the calendar: count the qualifying days the plan requires and map them against your availability. Then set the daily loss budget as the smaller of the plan's session stop and your own three-loss rule. Then set the per-trade risk so that five losses fit inside the daily budget. If the setup's stop distance forces a larger size than that, skip the trade; the plan is not asking you to take every valid setup, only to survive the ones you take.

During the attempt, record the remaining room and the daily loss used before each entry. After the session, update the high-water mark. A trader who can state both numbers from memory is far less likely to breach one by accident. The journal is not a diary; it is the risk dashboard the plan needs.

Which traders should avoid this product

A trader whose method needs a wide intraday range and frequent re-entries will find the session stop restrictive. A trader who cannot watch the US futures session should not buy a product built around it. A trader who has never traded a trailing threshold should paper-trade the rule for two weeks before paying, because the difference between a static and a trailing floor is larger than most marketing pages admit.

If the rulebook fits, Tradify's combination of a session stop and a trailing floor is a coherent product: the daily stop caps the damage, and the trailing floor forces discipline after a gain. The trader's job is to respect both, not to find a way around either.

Common questions

Does Tradify have a trailing or static floor?

The evaluation uses a trailing floor with a session stop. The failure point follows the equity high, while the daily stop caps a single session.

How many trades can I take in a day?

The plan does not set a fixed trade count. The daily stop and your per-trade risk determine how many losses the session can absorb; size so that a normal losing streak fits inside the daily budget.
