Challenge Fees vs Passing Service

A passing service is a product with a narrow scope: it trades an evaluation to its target under the firm’s rules and returns a funded account in your name. It is not a signal service, a managed fund or a guarantee. This guide turns the rule into something you can apply to the plan you are actually trading.

Key takeaways

  • The fee should be flat and the retry policy written down.
  • A written terms check per firm is the minimum standard.
  • No profit split means the funded account remains entirely yours.

The numbers that decide the cost

A passing service is a product with a narrow scope: it trades an evaluation to its target under the firm’s rules and returns a funded account in your name. It is not a signal service, a managed fund or a guarantee. The consequence is cumulative. Fees, retries, data costs and payout timing add up to a total cost of reaching the first withdrawal, and that total is the number worth comparing across routes.

The return on the fee, honestly

Compare expected cost, not sticker price

The evaluation fee is paid once per attempt, so the expected cost is the fee multiplied by the expected number of attempts. A $100 evaluation with a 40% pass probability has an expected cost of $250 before platform fees. A $220 passing service with a high completion rate has an expected cost of $220 plus any evaluation fee it does not cover. The comparison is not $100 against $220; it is the expected total against the expected total.

Time belongs in the same calculation. Three attempts over three months carry three months of platform costs, three months of waiting and three months in which the capital could be used elsewhere. A service that returns a funded account in two weeks is not just a different price; it is a different timeline. Put a dollar value on the time before deciding that the cheaper sticker is cheaper.

What the service fee buys and does not buy

The fee buys the work of passing the evaluation under the firm's rules. It does not buy a guarantee, a funded account that ignores the terms, or a profit split. A legitimate service returns the account in the client's name and does not take a share of the funded profit. If the offer includes a profit split, it is a different product with a different risk profile.

The fee also does not remove the evaluation fee unless the service says it does. Some services include the evaluation cost in the quote; others ask the client to buy the evaluation. Read the scope before comparing the numbers. A quote that looks cheaper may simply exclude a cost that the other quote includes.

The risk that no fee comparison captures

A passing service is only viable where the firm permits third-party trading. If the terms prohibit it, the account can be closed and the payout refused even if the evaluation was passed cleanly. That risk is not priced into either fee. Before choosing the service route, confirm the clause in writing and confirm that the permission covers the funded account, not just the evaluation.

If the clause is unclear, the self-trading route is the safer default. A flat fee is attractive, but it cannot compensate for a payout that never arrives.

Common questions

Is a $220 service cheaper than a $100 challenge?

Not necessarily. The challenge fee is paid per attempt, so the expected cost depends on the pass probability. Compare the expected total, including platform costs and time, rather than the sticker prices.

Does the service fee include the evaluation fee?

It depends on the quote. Some services include the evaluation cost and others ask the client to buy it. Read the scope before comparing totals.

What is the main risk of using a service?

The firm terms. If third-party trading is prohibited, the account can be closed and the payout refused. Confirm the clause in writing before paying.
