FTMO and FTUK are compared on the rows that decide an attempt: the loss floor, the daily stop, the billing rhythm and the payout conditions. They evaluate different markets — forex and CFD versus futures — which changes the sessions, the sizing and the gap risk before any headline target is relevant. FTMO vs FTUK: the two rulebooks side by side

The comparison that matters is not which firm has the larger target. It is which rulebook changes your behaviour. FTMO uses a static, measured from the starting balance floor, yes, equity-based daily stop and one-off per attempt; FTUK uses a trailing on peak equity floor, yes on most plans daily stop and one-off per attempt. Those differences decide how much you can risk, how fast you can pass and what a failed attempt costs.

FTMO evaluates forex and CFD accounts while FTUK evaluates futures accounts. That difference is prior to every other comparison: the sessions, position sizing, costs and gap risk are not the same product category. A trader who ignores this and compares only the headline target is comparing two different sports by their scoreboards.

The market row decides everything after it

FTMO evaluates forex and CFD accounts on weekday forex hours; FTUK evaluates futures on the US session. The instruments, the platform stack and the way a loss is measured are different, so a like-for-like comparison starts with the question of which market the trader can actually work. A forex trader who cannot be at the screen during US hours should not buy the futures product, and a futures trader who needs a data feed should not assume the forex platform list applies.

FTMO uses a static percentage floor measured from the starting balance and an equity daily stop. FTUK uses a trailing threshold on peak equity with a daily stop on most plans. The static floor keeps the original dollar distance available for the whole attempt; the trailing floor reduces it after every new high. That is the row that changes position size.

A $100,000 account on each rulebook

On the FTMO account, a 10% floor puts the failure point at $90,000, and a $500 risk unit has twenty losses of room. On the FTUK account, a $3,000 trailing threshold gives six losses at the same size. After a $1,000 gain, the FTUK floor moves up and the room from the original balance falls to $2,000, or four losses at $500. Reducing to $250 restores eight.

The target math is separate. A 10% target on the forex account needs $10,000; a futures target expressed in dollars or points needs to be converted at the contract's value per point. A trader who compares the two targets without converting them is comparing a currency amount with a distance on a chart.

Both firms bill once per attempt on the plans compared here, so the calendar does not add fees. The trailing floor is the main driver of retries, and each retry is a full fee.

Who should choose which

FTMO fits a forex trader who wants a static floor, the widest platform list and a conventional verification path. FTUK fits a futures trader who wants a one-off purchase, TradingView access and a trailing-threshold challenge. A trader who values a fixed failure point should choose the forex product; a trader who values a one-off fee and can trade futures should choose FTUK.

If both markets are available to you, compare the size tables you would actually use. The account that lets you keep a normal risk unit without breaching on a retracement is the one that fits, regardless of which brand is cheaper at checkout.
