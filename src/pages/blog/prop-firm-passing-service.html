The product in one paragraph

A prop firm passing service trades an evaluation on the client's behalf for a flat fee and returns a funded account in the client's name. It is not a signal service, a managed fund or a guarantee. The service reads the rulebook, builds a risk plan, executes the evaluation and reports progress. The client keeps ownership of the account and the profit.

The arrangement is only legitimate where the firm permits third-party trading. That is the first question to ask, and the answer should be in writing. A service that treats the terms check as a formality is a service that has not understood the risk.

What the fee should include

A flat fee should cover the work, the risk plan and the retry policy stated in the quote. It should not include a share of the funded profit, and it should not require the client to hand over a personal account with their own capital. The evaluation fee may or may not be included; the quote should say which.

Retries are the part of the fee that matters most. A service that charges a full fee for every attempt is effectively a per-attempt product; a service that includes retries is pricing the risk of failure into the initial fee. Neither is automatically better, but the policy should be written down and the expected number of attempts should be part of the comparison.

Who the service fits

It fits a trader who values time, has a firm that permits the arrangement and does not want to manage the evaluation personally. It does not fit a trader who wants to learn the rulebook, or one whose firm prohibits third-party trading, or one who expects the service to trade the funded account afterward. The service ends at the pass; the funded account is the client's responsibility.

Before paying, confirm the terms, the fee, the retry policy and the timeline. If any answer is vague, the service is not ready.

It depends on the firm terms. The arrangement is legitimate only where the firm permits third-party trading. A prohibited arrangement can lead to a closed account and a refused payout.
