What this guide is really solving

What to Do After Passing Prop Firm Challenge is one of the decisions that sits between a trading method and a funded account. It changes how much you can risk, how quickly you can pass, or what a pass is worth after the fact, and it is usually decided by a clause rather than by the market. On an evaluation, the consequence is specific: the same decision either keeps the loss floor at a distance or brings the end of the account closer. The sections below turn that into a plan rather than a principle.

The first week after the pass

The pass changes the account status, not the risk. The first week is for confirming the funded terms, the payout schedule, the consistency condition and the platform access. Read the funded agreement before placing a trade; it can differ from the evaluation terms in exactly the places that matter. Do not assume the rules you learned during the challenge are the rules that apply now.

The first week is also for setting a new baseline. The funded account may have a different loss floor, a different daily stop and a different target. Recalculate the risk unit from the new numbers. A trader who carries the evaluation size into the funded account without checking the floor is taking an unmeasured risk.

The first payout is a process, not a payday

Plan the first withdrawal before the first funded trade. Confirm the qualifying period, the minimum withdrawal, the processor fee and the account balance that must remain after the payout. A withdrawal that leaves the account too close to the floor can affect the next cycle. Take a smaller first payout if it preserves the buffer.

Keep the statement and the support reply. The first payout teaches the firm's rhythm; the second is where the income becomes predictable. A trader who treats the first payout as the goal often withdraws too much and restarts the risk cycle with less room.

Scaling without losing the account

After two or three clean payouts, consider scaling. Increase size gradually, not in one jump. The target grows with the account, but the edge does not automatically grow with it. Test the larger size on a demo or a small live account before applying it to the funded account. If the plan has a formal scaling schedule, read the conditions and the review time.

The account is a business asset, not a lottery ticket. The goal is to keep it for many cycles, not to maximize one. A trader who protects the floor and withdraws regularly will outperform one who swings for a large month and loses the account.

Common questions

What should I do immediately after passing?

Read the funded terms, confirm the payout schedule and recalculate the risk unit from the new floor. The evaluation rules may differ from the funded rules.

How much should I withdraw first?

An amount that leaves a comfortable buffer above the floor and satisfies the plan’s minimum-balance condition. A smaller first payout preserves the account for the next cycle.

When should I scale?

After two or three clean payouts and a stable process. Increase gradually and test the larger size before applying it to the funded account.
