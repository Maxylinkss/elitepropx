FundedNext Stellar rules in cash

Stellar is a two-step plan with the same shape as most of the market and two differences that matter: a smaller first target, and a consistency term that is confirmed at checkout rather than printed on the plan page. Here is the rulebook at $100,000.

The smaller first target is the real difference

Eight percent instead of ten is not a rounding difference over a phase. On a $100,000 account it removes $2,000 of profit requirement from the phase most traders fail, and it changes the relationship between the target and the daily limit: the target is 1.6 daily budgets rather than 2, so the required pace is achievable with fewer sessions and less size per session.

Whether that is genuinely easier depends on what replaces it. A five-day minimum instead of four adds a session to protect, and a consistency clause confirmed at checkout can constrain how the profit is allowed to arrive. A plan that is easier to reach and harder to withdraw from is not obviously better; it depends on which stage you expect to struggle at.

The clause that is not on the plan page

The consistency term being "confirm at checkout" is the one line worth chasing before you pay. Consistency rules usually compare your best day's profit to the total or to the target, which means a single outsized session can leave an otherwise passed evaluation unable to withdraw. Because the term is not printed alongside the targets, a trader who read the plan summary has not read the rule that decides the payout.

The practical response is to buy with the rule open rather than to guess: put the question to support in writing, keep the reply, and plan the evaluation so no single day dominates the total. A pass spread across ordinary sessions satisfies almost every version of the clause, which is why the safer target is a steady curve rather than a fast one.

What to confirm before you pay

  • The model. Stellar is two-phase; other FundedNext products are one-phase with a different target and a consistency rule. The names are not interchangeable.
  • The consistency term in writing. Ask what it compares, at which stage it applies, and whether it constrains the first payout only.
  • The daily measurement basis. Confirm equity versus closed balance and the reset time, because the same 5% means different room under each.
  • The third-party trading clause. Nothing on this page establishes that another person may trade your evaluation; that is a separate permission and it decides whether the funded account survives a payout request.

Planning against these numbers

With an $8,000 first target and a $5,000 daily budget, the temptation is to take a third of the budget per trade and finish quickly. The five-day minimum makes that pointless: the fastest legal finish is day five, so the size that reaches $8,000 in five sessions is roughly $1,600 a day, which is under a third of the budget and survivable across a bad session.

Frequently asked

Is the FundedNext Stellar target 8% or 10%?

The Stellar 2-Step publishes 8% for phase one and 5% for phase two. Other FundedNext models use different figures, so check the model name on the plan you are buying rather than the brand.

Does Stellar have a consistency rule?

The plan summary says to confirm it at checkout rather than printing the term. That is exactly the sentence to get in writing from support before paying, because a consistency clause enforced at payout can stop a passed evaluation from withdrawing.

How many trading days are required?

Five, one more than the four-day minimum common on two-step plans. It is not a long delay, but it means the fastest possible finish is day five and the target should be paced across at least that many sessions.

Is the maximum loss static?

According to the published CFD objectives, yes — a static 10% measured from the starting balance. Confirm on the specific plan page, because trailing structures appear on some FundedNext products.
