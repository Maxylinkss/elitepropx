Why these five

Because these are the plans whose figures were read from the firm's own rulebook and dated. There is no value in a directory that lists forty firms when the numbers for thirty-five of them were copied from another comparison site two years ago. Five verified rulebooks beat forty guesses, and unconfirmed rows here say so rather than pretending.

It also matters that the five cover four different structures. Two are static with a two-phase target, two are futures evaluations with dollar targets and a trailing floor, and one counts profitable days instead of days. If you can read those four structures you can read almost any plan on the market, because the differences that decide outcomes are structural rather than cosmetic.

What actually separates them

The headline target is the least useful number on any of these pages. What decides an evaluation is the drawdown model — static, end-of-day trailing or intraday trailing — followed by the daily limit, and then the calendar in the form of minimum days. Two firms with an identical 10% target can be wildly different assignments for the same strategy, and a trader can pass one and fail the other with an unchanged trading plan.

The second separator is the consistency clause, and specifically the stage it applies at. FundedNext asks you to confirm it at checkout. Apex enforces it at payout. Topstep enforces 55% during the Combine. The5ers states none. That single difference changes whether a good week is an asset or a liability.

What these pages are not

They are not reviews. Whether a firm pays on time, how quickly support responds and what the funded phase feels like are separate questions, covered in the firm guides linked from each page. And they are not a statement that any firm permits a third-party trader on an evaluation.

That last point is worth stating plainly: the rules below tell you how an evaluation is scored, not whether it is allowed to be traded by someone else. Some firms prohibit it outright, and a pass on a prohibited account produces a funded account that can be voided at the first payout request. Check the clause, in writing, before paying anyone — including us. Our risk disclosure sets out what that exposure actually is.

Frequently asked

Are these figures current?

Each page carries the date its figures were read from the firm's documentation, and the source links are listed at the foot. Firms revise targets, drawdown models and consistency terms without notice, so confirm anything you are about to pay a fee against.

Why are some numbers missing?

Because they are published per account size and were not independently confirmed in this pass. A missing figure is more useful than one copied from a different size or a legacy plan.

Which of these is easiest to pass?

It depends on the strategy rather than on the firm. A static floor with no consistency rule is the most forgiving structure. A trailing floor removes room as profit accumulates and demands steadily smaller size, which is a different skill from making a target.

Does ElitePropX trade evaluations at all five firms?
